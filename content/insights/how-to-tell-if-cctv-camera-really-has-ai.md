## "AI" on the box is not the same as AI on your site

Many cameras and recorders now carry an "AI" label on the box. In practice the word can describe very different things: a basic motion filter, a person and vehicle detector, or a full analytics system with rules and reports.

You do not need to be technical to find out which one you are buying. You need good questions. If you want the broader picture first, read [AI CCTV vs normal CCTV](/insights/ai-cctv-vs-normal-cctv).

## 8 questions to ask the seller

### 1. What exactly does it detect?

Ask for the list. A real feature has a name: person, vehicle, a crossed line, an object left behind. "Smart detection" with no list is a warning sign.

### 2. Is that motion detection or object detection?

Motion detection reacts to changing pixels. Shadows, rain, insects and headlights can trigger it. Object detection tries to recognise what is in the frame. Ask which one raises the alert, and whether you can switch between them.

### 3. Where does the processing happen?

It may run inside the camera, in the recorder, on a separate analytics unit, or on a server. Each choice has limits. In-camera features are simple but fixed. A separate unit can add more, but it is another device to support. See [what video analytics software is](/insights/what-is-video-analytics-software) for the general idea.

### 4. Will it work on my cameras and my scene?

A demo on the seller's own camera proves little. Ask for a trial on your site, with your angles and your night light. Check [where to place cameras for AI detection](/insights/where-to-place-cctv-cameras-for-ai-detection) before the trial so the test is fair.

### 5. What happens with false alerts?

Every detection system produces some wrong alerts. Ask how you can tune it, who tunes it, and how long that takes. Our guide on [reducing AI CCTV false alarms](/insights/ai-cctv-false-alarms-how-to-reduce) lists what to look for.

### 6. Does it need the internet?

Some features run locally. Others need a cloud connection. Ask what stops working when the link drops, and what is kept until it comes back. Read [whether AI CCTV works without internet](/insights/does-ai-cctv-work-without-internet).

### 7. Where do the alerts go, and who acts on them?

An alert nobody reads is only a log. Ask how alerts are delivered, to whom, and what the person should do. If there is no answer, the feature is not ready for daily use.

### 8. What is included in support and in the price?

Ask what is included now and what costs extra later: licences, extra camera channels, storage, updates and support visits. Never accept a verbal promise in place of a written scope. See [what AI CCTV should cost](/insights/ai-cctv-price-in-india-what-it-should-cost) for the cost items worth listing.

## Signs the "AI" claim is thin

- No list of detection types.
- No way to see or change detection settings.
- Only a demo video, never a test on your cameras.
- Promises of perfect accuracy or no false alarms. No honest product can promise that.
- No written description of what is included.

## Do the simplest test yourself

Pick one camera and one situation that matters to you, such as a person entering a gate after hours. Ask the seller to set it up, trigger it in front of you, and show you the alert and the saved clip. Then repeat it at night.

If it works, you know more than any brochure can tell you. If it does not, you have learned that cheaply.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I tell if a CCTV camera really uses AI?
Ask what it detects, where the detection runs, and ask for a live test on your own scene. A real feature has a named object class, a setting to configure and a result you can see. Vague words like smart or intelligent are not proof.

### Is motion detection the same as AI?
No. Basic motion detection reacts to pixels changing, so shadows, rain and insects can trigger it. Object detection tries to recognise a person or vehicle. Some products use both, so ask which one raises the alert.

### Should the AI run in the camera or on a separate unit?
Either can work. In-camera detection is simple but limited to what the camera offers. A separate unit or server can be more flexible. Ask the seller to explain the limits of their choice for your site.
