## Two names, one confused buyer

You ask three vendors for an attendance system. One says "digital attendance". One says "time and attendance". A third says "biometric". Their quotes look alike, but are they selling the same thing?

Mostly they are. Many vendors use the two names for one product, and the difference people notice is usually the method of capture. This guide separates the words so you can ask better questions.

## What "time attendance" means

Time attendance is the job: recording when each person starts and stops work. The basic record is a clock-in time and a clock-out time.

From those two times a system can work out hours worked, late arrival, early exit and overtime. That is the part payroll needs.

## What "digital attendance" means

Digital attendance describes how the record is made. Instead of a paper register or a signed sheet, a device or an app captures the entry and stores it in software.

So "digital" is a wide label. A card-swipe machine, a fingerprint terminal, a mobile app and a face-recognition camera can all be called digital attendance. Each captures the entry differently.

## What each method really records

This is the part that matters. Whatever the label, ask what the system records at the moment of capture.

- **Card or PIN.** It records that a card or code was used. It does not record who used it. This is how [proxy attendance](/insights/how-to-stop-proxy-attendance) happens.
- **Fingerprint terminal.** It records that a finger matched a stored template. It is tied to a person, but worn fingers, dust and oil cause failures. See [why fingerprint attendance fails](/insights/fingerprint-attendance-system-why-it-fails).
- **Mobile app.** It records that a phone sent a check-in, sometimes with a location. It records the phone, not always the person holding it. See [geofencing for field staff](/insights/geofencing-attendance-field-staff).
- **Camera-based.** It records that an enrolled person was seen by a camera at a time and place. The result depends on the camera view and lighting.

## Where a time attendance system stops

Capture is only the start. The rest of a good system sits behind it:

1. **Rules.** Shifts, grace periods, half-days, overtime and holidays.
2. **Corrections.** A way for a supervisor to fix a missed punch, with a note of who changed what.
3. **Leave.** Whether leave links to attendance. See [attendance and leave management](/insights/attendance-leave-management-integration).
4. **Payroll output.** An export your payroll can read, or a direct link.

Many wrong purchases come from buying a good terminal and a weak rulebook. The device is easy to judge. The rules take more thought.

## Presence is not the same as a punch

A time record says someone punched at 9:02. It does not say the person worked until 5:30, or stood at the right post. Some businesses do not need more than the punch. A small office with trusted staff may be happy with that.

A factory gate, a contract labour site or a warehouse often wants a stronger link between the person and the record. That is where camera-based methods are discussed. If you have cameras already, see [replacing the attendance register with a camera](/insights/replace-attendance-register-with-camera) and [face recognition attendance vs a biometric machine](/insights/face-recognition-attendance-vs-biometric-machine).

## Questions to ask any vendor

1. What exactly is recorded when a person marks attendance?
2. How do you stop one person marking for another?
3. How are missed or wrong entries corrected, and is each change logged?
4. What happens if the device or internet is down for an hour?
5. Where is the data kept, for how long, and who can see it?
6. What can I export, and can I take my history with me if I switch?

For a longer list, use [what to check before buying an attendance system](/insights/what-to-check-before-buying-attendance-system). If you are covered by record-keeping rules, read [attendance records law in India](/insights/attendance-records-law-india) too.

## A note on camera-based attendance

If you want to try attendance from cameras you already have, treat it as a trial. The camera angle, distance and light decide whether it can work at your door. People also need to be told and enrolled with consent, and there must be a human way to correct mistakes. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a digital attendance system the same as a time attendance system?

Often the two names are used for the same product. Strictly, 'time attendance' describes the job: recording when people start and stop work. 'Digital' describes how it is done: software instead of a paper register. Ask the vendor exactly what gets recorded and how.

### What does a time attendance system record?

At its simplest, a time attendance system records a clock-in and clock-out time for each person. Better ones also keep shift, late arrival, early exit, overtime and leave. It records that someone punched or scanned, not necessarily that the person was at work.

### Can attendance be recorded from existing CCTV cameras?

Sometimes. A camera-based method records that an enrolled person was seen at a place and time, if the camera view suits it. It needs a feasibility check on your own cameras, consent and a clear way to correct wrong or missing records.
