## You need the footage, and the clock is running

A guest loses a laptop from a hotel room corridor. A family says a bag was taken from the lobby. A traveller wants to know who entered a locker room. The first thought is the same: "The hotel must have it on CCTV."

Often it does. But the footage may not stay for long, and the hotel may not hand it to you directly. This guide explains who decides, what to ask for and in what order.

## Who decides what happens to hotel footage

The hotel runs the cameras, so the hotel controls the recorder. In most cases, it is the hotel's decision whether to show a clip to a guest. There is no simple rule we could confirm that forces a private hotel to give a copy to any guest who asks.

There is also a privacy side. A lobby clip shows many people, not only you. That is why many hotels will release footage to the police or to a court, and are careful about giving it to an individual. This is a general explanation, not legal advice. For a serious matter, speak to a lawyer.

## Footage can be overwritten quickly

A CCTV recorder stores video on a disk. When the disk fills, the newest video replaces the oldest. How long that takes depends on the number of cameras, the video quality and the disk size. Our guide on [how many days of CCTV storage you actually get](/insights/cctv-storage-how-many-days) shows how to work it out.

News reports on Indian cases show the same pattern. In one Delhi case, hotel staff told police that footage was automatically erased after a month. We could not confirm a fixed period that applies to every hotel, so assume the clip may be gone within weeks.

Some local police have given directions to hotels and similar places on keeping footage. Check the rules of your city or district. Do not assume that one number applies everywhere.

## What to do in the first hour

Speed matters more than anything else. Do these in order:

1. **Note the facts.** Write down the date, the exact time window, the floor or area, and what is missing or what happened.
2. **Speak to the manager or security head.** Ask them to preserve the footage for that window. Say "preserve", not only "show".
3. **Put it in writing.** Send an email or a letter the same day. Keep a copy, and note the name of the person you spoke to.
4. **Ask what cameras cover the area.** Do not argue about the footage on the spot. A calm, specific request works better.
5. **Report to the police if a crime is involved.** A theft, assault or harassment case should go to the police, who can formally ask the hotel for the recording.

## Why the police route matters

If you only ask the hotel, the hotel may say no, or may say the footage is gone. If you file a complaint, the police can ask the hotel to produce and hand over the recording. For use in a case, the way the copy is made and kept also matters. Our post on [CCTV footage as evidence in India](/insights/cctv-footage-legal-evidence-india) explains why the original and a record of how it was copied should be kept.

In a case before the Chandigarh courts, the court directed the police to preserve the CCTV footage of a hotel for a particular period. Courts can order preservation, which is another reason to involve the police early instead of waiting.

## What a hotel can do better

If you run a hotel or guest house, this question will come to you. Prepare before it does:

- decide how many days of footage you keep, and know why,
- write a short policy on who may view or export footage,
- keep a log of every export, with the date, the person and the reason,
- check every camera regularly, so a "no footage" answer is never the result of a dead camera,
- place signs where cameras operate, as explained in [CCTV signage in India](/insights/cctv-signage-requirements-india).

A hotel that can answer within a day, with a clear process, builds trust with guests and with the police. Our post on [hotel security and staff attendance](/insights/hotel-security-and-staff-attendance) covers other basics for hospitality sites.

## What guests should not do

Do not ask staff to let you take photos of the monitor with your phone without permission. Do not post a clip online. Do not accuse a named person without evidence. Each of these can harm your case or cause a dispute. Stay factual and let the proper process work.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a hotel give CCTV footage to a guest?

A hotel may show or share footage at its own discretion, but it is not usually bound to hand it to a guest on request. Footage also shows other guests, so many hotels release it only to the police or on a court order. Ask in writing and ask them to preserve it.

### How long do hotels keep CCTV footage in India?

There is no single period that we could confirm for every hotel. It depends on disk size, camera count and the hotel's own settings, and some local police directions ask for a set period. Assume footage can be overwritten within weeks and ask for it to be saved at once.

### What should I do first if something happened at a hotel?

Tell the hotel manager in writing, note the camera area and the exact time, and ask them to preserve the footage. If a crime or theft is involved, file a complaint with the police, who can ask the hotel to hand over the recording.
