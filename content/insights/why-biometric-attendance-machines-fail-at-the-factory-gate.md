Every factory owner has seen this scene: it's 8:58 a.m., the shift starts at 9:00, and forty workers are queued at a single fingerprint machine. The line moves one beep at a time. Some fingers scan on the first try. Many don't.

The machine was supposed to end attendance headaches. Instead it created a new ritual.

## Where fingerprint machines struggle

Biometric machines are fine in an air-conditioned office. A factory gate is a different world:

- **Worn and dusty fingers.** Grinding, loading, oil, cement — manual work erases fingerprints faster than sensors can read them. The worker tries three times, wipes their hand, tries again. The queue grows.
- **Shift-change pileups.** One machine, one scan at a time. When 100 people arrive in the same ten minutes, you either buy more machines or accept that "9:00" really means "9:20".
- **Gloves, safety gear, wet hands.** Anything between skin and sensor means a failed scan.
- **The machine itself.** It sits at the gate — heat, dust and humidity are not kind to it. When it dies, attendance goes back to the notebook.
- **The register still lies.** Machines log a scan, not a person's day. Someone scans in, leaves for three hours, comes back — the register shows a full day.

None of this is the manufacturer's fault. It's just the wrong tool for a gate that a hundred people cross at once.

## The camera at your gate already sees everyone

Here's the thing: you almost certainly have a camera pointed at that gate already. Every worker who walks in passes through its frame, every single day. No queue, no touching, no scanning ritual — they just walk.

Face-recognition attendance uses that camera as the attendance machine:

- **Workers simply walk through.** The camera identifies each face as people pass. A hundred people can enter in the time one fingerprint queue clears.
- **Nothing to touch, nothing to wear off.** Dust, gloves and wet hands don't matter.
- **Proxy punching gets hard.** A friend can carry your fingerprint card or know your PIN. Carrying your face is harder.
- **The same system sees the day, not just the entry.** Because it's built on cameras, presence during the shift — not just the 9:00 beep — becomes visible.
- **Payroll gets a clean feed.** In/out times flow into a register automatically, instead of being typed from a machine export that HR still has to correct.

## What it takes to do this well

Honest caveat: not every camera is ready for this on day one. Face recognition needs the face to be seen properly — a camera mounted very high, looking at the tops of heads, won't identify anyone reliably. Usually the fix is small: one well-placed camera at the choke point where people naturally walk through, at closer to face height, with decent light.

That's a one-time adjustment — after which your gate takes attendance by itself, every day, with no queue and no maintenance ritual.
