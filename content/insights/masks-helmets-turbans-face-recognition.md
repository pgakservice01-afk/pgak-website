Your team wears masks, helmets, turbans or other headwear during normal work. A face-attendance proposal is only useful if it is evaluated with the real process in mind. A demonstration that depends on removing required protection does not establish suitability for the workplace.

Keep safety requirements and respectful treatment of employees at the centre of the assessment. The task is to find an appropriate attendance method, not to make workers change essential equipment or permitted dress to rescue a sales claim.

## Define the actual attendance location

Identify where the record is supposed to be captured and what equipment must be worn there. Ask the responsible site owner to confirm the permitted route, stopping behaviour and any safety constraints. Do not assume a gate, corridor or area outside a machine is automatically a safe place to remove protection.

Where PPE is required, it stays on during the trial. Do not ask someone to lift a visor, remove a respirator or loosen protective equipment to produce a better image. If the proposed method does not work adequately under the required conditions, assess another approved method.

Separate this decision from production entry permission. An attendance record should not create an exception to the site's established safety process.

## Avoid blanket promises about headwear

The relevant question is what the selected implementation can handle in the actual view, not whether a garment appears on a generic list. Camera angle, lighting, movement and the portion of the face visible should be assessed alongside the system's requirements.

NIST's [2020 masked-face recognition study](https://www.nist.gov/publications/ongoing-face-recognition-vendor-test-frvt-part-6b-face-recognition-accuracy-face-masks) evaluated particular algorithms and test conditions. It is historical benchmark evidence, not proof that masks always work or never work in a current PGAK installation.

Treat turbans, helmets and other permitted headwear respectfully. Do not generalize a limited trial to all people who wear a particular item or make claims about a demographic group's recognition performance.

## Use the ordinary process in the trial

Agree an authorized sample of normal arrivals and relevant shifts. Include the actual equipment combinations used for those tasks, with appropriate notice, permission and handling of the resulting images. Record any condition the trial does not cover.

Review whether the intended camera feed supplies a usable capture and whether the proposed system produces the expected identity result. Keep missed and incorrect matches separate. The [accuracy evaluation guide](/insights/face-recognition-accuracy-what-affects-it) explains why a single success percentage is insufficient.

Do not repeatedly coach a participant into an unusual pose and then describe the result as normal hands-free operation. If a pause or specific interaction is required, document it as part of the workflow and assess its practicality.

## Check enrolment without assuming a universal recipe

Follow the selected system's supported enrolment procedure. Confirm how it handles the intended operating appearance and whether it permits additional samples or another enrollment method. Do not assume that collecting more photographs is always supported or fixes every problem.

The [enrolment checklist](/insights/how-many-photos-face-enrolment) covers identity mapping and validation. Keep enrolment changes authorized and traceable, and retest the real operating conditions after any approved change.

If a camera adjustment is proposed, involve the responsible installer. A change must preserve safe access and the required view; it should not quietly compromise another recording purpose.

## Establish an approved exception path

Agree how a person receives an attendance record when the intended method cannot produce a reliable result. A supervisor-reviewed record or another credential method may be appropriate only if the organization approves it and the chosen system supports it.

Document who reviews the exception, what information is needed and how corrections are kept traceable. Do not infer absence or make a pay-related decision solely from an unresolved recognition event. Review repeated problems to decide whether the method itself needs changing.

For an AI Alerto by PGAK assessment, bring the location, actual PPE requirements and attendance process. The proposal should state tested conditions, known limits and the supported fallback. Keeping required protection on is a requirement of the assessment, not an obstacle to be removed.

## Questions before the assessment

### Should workers remove required masks or visors for face attendance?

No. Required protective equipment must stay in place wherever the safety procedure requires it. Assess the attendance method under those conditions or provide an approved alternative. A successful demonstration does not justify weakening a safety control.

### Will turbans or helmets always work with face recognition?

There is no universal result for every system and setting. Test the actual permitted headwear, camera view, lighting and movement with authorized participants. Document limitations without treating a group or a garment as inherently unsuitable.
