## A working live view is not proof that your CCTV system is working

A CCTV estate can look fine on a monitor while one camera has stopped recording, the clock is wrong, the storage has shrunk, or the night view is too dark to be useful. Those faults usually surface only when someone needs a clip.

A short, repeatable check prevents that. It also gives a business a clean record of what was tested, what was fixed and what still needs attention.

This checklist is for existing cameras, DVRs or NVRs. It is not a substitute for safe electrical work or manufacturer-specific service instructions.

## The 12-point CCTV maintenance checklist

### 1. Check every camera has a clear, stable image

Open each important feed. Look for haze, dirt, cobwebs, a shifted angle, water ingress, flicker or a blocked view. Compare the image with the area the camera is meant to cover—not simply whether a picture exists.

### 2. Check the camera's purpose still matches the scene

Warehouses change. Pallets, racks, banners and parked vehicles can block a view that was clear when the system was installed. At entrances, ask whether the view shows a person usefully, rather than only the top of their head.

### 3. Inspect the view after dark

A camera that performs in daylight may be weak at night. Check glare from headlights, reflections from nearby walls, infrared bounce from glass or rain, and dark areas around the actual gate, shutter or path you care about.

### 4. Confirm recording on the recorder

Open the DVR or NVR's status page and check that the expected channels are recording. Do not rely on a live multiview screen as evidence that clips are being stored.

### 5. Prove playback for a recent time window

Choose a recent time, play it back and confirm video is present and usable. This catches recording gaps that a live feed will hide.

### 6. Check the retention period in practice

Find the oldest available clip on a priority camera. Compare it with the retention period your business expects. Adding cameras or increasing resolution can quietly shorten the period without an obvious alarm.

### 7. Review disk and recorder health

Use the recorder's own health information for disk warnings, abnormal recording status or capacity issues. Escalate a disk warning to the support provider; do not wait for an incident to test whether it mattered.

### 8. Check date and time on footage

Compare the recorder time with a trusted current time. A wrong timestamp can make incident review, staff handover and event correlation unnecessarily difficult.

### 9. Check power continuity

Confirm that cameras, PoE switches or injectors, recorder and—if remote viewing is needed—the router are covered by the intended power plan. Then schedule a safe test; see the [CCTV power backup checklist](/insights/cctv-power-backup-checklist).

### 10. Check cables, mounts and outdoor housings

Look for loose mounts, exposed or damaged cable, corrosion, cracked glands and signs of water or pest damage. This is a visual inspection, not permission to open electrical enclosures unless the appropriate technician is assigned.

### 11. Check authorised remote access

If a manager needs remote viewing, confirm it works for the authorised user. Remove access for leavers and avoid sharing a single generic login. Access controls are part of camera maintenance, not an admin afterthought.

### 12. Write down the result and owner

Record the camera or zone, result, fault, action owner and target date. A checklist only becomes useful when unresolved items have someone responsible for closing them.

## When to involve a specialist

Call the service provider when there is persistent loss of video, a storage or disk warning, water ingress, unstable power, a damaged mount or a view that no longer supports the required task. Resetting a device or changing firmware without a record can create a bigger outage.

A good maintenance plan is different from a vague promise to “attend when called.” For what to ask for in a service arrangement, read [what a CCTV AMC should include](/insights/cctv-amc-what-should-it-include).

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should a CCTV system be checked?

Agree a documented check interval for the site's equipment, exposure and service agreement. Include recording and playback checks, and review the schedule after faults or system changes.

### What is the most important CCTV maintenance check?

Confirming playback is often the most valuable check because a live picture alone does not prove that the recorder is saving usable footage for the required period.
