Two CCTV quotes can list the same camera count and describe very different jobs. One may include cable routes, storage, commissioning and support. Another may leave those items to be priced after work begins.

Choose an installer by the system they agree to deliver and the evidence they will provide at handover. A low per-camera price is difficult to compare until the scope is the same.

## Give every bidder the same site brief

Describe the entrances, loading areas, restricted spaces and existing equipment. Write the purpose of each proposed view: overview, a clear record at a handover point, or an event that needs timely attention. Include working hours and night conditions.

Ask whether a site survey is needed before the quote becomes firm. Mounting access, cable routes, power, network ownership and operating restrictions can change the work. Identify who approves drilling, elevated work and any planned interruption to existing coverage.

For a warehouse, use a [coverage planning checklist](/insights/how-many-cameras-does-a-warehouse-need) before agreeing a camera count. It is easier to compare bids when each proposed camera has a stated job.

## Ask for an itemised scope

| Quote item | Detail to request |
| --- | --- |
| Cameras and recorder | Exact models, quantities and the role of each view |
| Installation | Mounting, cable type and routes, termination, protection and exclusions |
| Power and network | Switches, power budget, backup scope and responsibility for existing infrastructure |
| Recording | Required retention, calculation inputs, storage and recording settings |
| Software | Included features, licence term, channel limits and renewal items |
| Support | Warranty process, service area, contact route and maintenance scope |

Also separate taxes, optional work and assumptions. Ask how variations are approved before installation. If a quoted model changes, require confirmation that its relevant performance and integration requirements still match the agreed scope.

This is a comparison worksheet, not a standard price list. A meaningful total depends on the site and the work included.

## Check the evidence behind the proposal

Ask to see sample footage relevant to your conditions and explain what you need to recognise in it. A sharp daytime image does not settle a night-time gate requirement. Check the recording or export that you will actually use, not only a live preview.

References can help establish how an installer handles commissioning and faults. Request permissioned examples or a reference contact; do not ask a contractor to open another customer's live cameras without authorisation.

For AI analytics, request a model-specific integration check and a proposed pilot. Brand names and an ONVIF label alone do not approve the complete setup. Read the [compatibility checklist](/platform/compatibility) and agree which events the test must include.

## Put acceptance into the work order

Before installation, agree how you will verify each camera's view, day and required night conditions, recording, export and time settings. Include the planned retention and any analytics or alert-delivery tests in scope.

At handover, keep a location-labelled channel list, equipment inventory, configuration record and unresolved-issues list. Confirm how the authorised business owner receives administrator access and who can make changes afterwards. Credentials should be transferred through a secure process, not printed on a public notice or left in an open shared document.

If the installer promises remote support, document how access is approved and disabled. The arrangement should remain understandable when the employee who commissioned the system leaves.

## Compare support as carefully as hardware

“Support included” needs a definition. Distinguish the time to acknowledge a fault, attend the site and restore service. Ask which hours count, which locations are covered and what happens while a replacement part is unavailable.

Do not assume that a local firm is always faster or a large firm is always more consistent. Ask for the actual service arrangement for your site. The [CCTV AMC checklist](/insights/cctv-amc-what-should-it-include) provides questions for the maintenance stage.

For a PGAK discussion, prepare your city, site type, approximate camera count and the business problem you want to solve. An AI Alerto assessment can clarify the analytics requirement; installation availability and the final technical scope must be confirmed for your location.
