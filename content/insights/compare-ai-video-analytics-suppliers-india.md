**Compare AI video analytics suppliers using the same camera inventory, event definitions, pilot conditions and cost scope.** A brand list, an accuracy percentage or a low starting price is not enough to decide whether a system suits your factory, warehouse or shop.

PGAK is a supplier, so this guide is a buying framework with a disclosed commercial interest. Apply the same questions to us.

## What should every supplier receive?

Prepare a brief with sites, camera/recorder models, firmware, selected channels, operating hours and the events that matter. Name the person who will act on alerts and any required VMS, attendance or gate-system integration. Agree how footage can be shared securely; do not include passwords in an enquiry form.

Use the same information for each supplier. Otherwise one proposal may cover basic object detection while another includes hardware, commissioning and an ongoing response workflow.

## Which evidence should you compare?

| Criterion | Evidence to request |
|---|---|
| Compatibility | Exact model/firmware/stream configuration and representative test results |
| Detection | Defined events, true/false/missed counts, sample size and conditions |
| Reliability | Outage, reconnection, restart and event-delivery tests |
| Integration | Demonstration of the required workflow, with unsupported steps named |
| Cost | Itemised first-year and renewal cost, tax, hardware ownership and exclusions |
| Support | Named responsibilities, hours, response targets and escalation |
| Data and exit | Access controls, retention, export, deletion and termination process |

Set any must-have requirements before scoring. A strong average cannot compensate for a critical integration that does not work or an unacceptable safety/access condition.

## How do you score a pilot fairly?

One simple **proposed procurement scale**, not an industry standard, is:

- **0:** not demonstrated or fails the agreed requirement.
- **1:** works partly, with material unresolved limitations.
- **2:** meets the agreed requirement in the observed test.

Assign weights according to your site's priorities and record the evidence beside each score. For example, night intrusion and alert delivery may matter more than a broad analytics catalogue at an unattended warehouse. Keep commercial scoring separate from technical pass/fail decisions.

The final record should identify the assessor, date, configuration and unresolved issues. Do not award a score because a feature is listed on a website.

## What do published competitor materials help you check?

Supplier sites can help build questions, but they are not independent validation:

- [Staqu's JK Cement case study](https://www.staqu.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/JK-Cement-Case-Study.pdf) gives a named industrial setting to examine. Ask for the method and conditions behind any result.
- [Videonetics' manufacturing page](https://www.videonetics.com/index.php/manufacturing) helps frame industrial use cases. Confirm the particular workflows in your proposed deployment.
- [AllGoVision's technical FAQ](https://www.allgovision.com/faqs.php) raises camera and VMS compatibility questions. Reconfirm support for your exact versions.
- [Vianix](https://vianix.ai/) describes deployment and retrofit options. Ask which option, dependencies and support terms your quote includes.

These are examples of primary supplier material checked for this guide, not a ranking or endorsement. No competitor traffic, independent accuracy ranking or current price comparison is claimed.

## What should a price comparison include?

Distinguish software-only licences from camera packages and managed deployments. Compare selected channels, processing hardware, setup, integration, support and renewal terms. Use the worked arithmetic in [AI CCTV cost in India](/insights/ai-cctv-price-in-india-what-it-should-cost) rather than multiplying an unverified “market rate”.

A cheaper quote may cover a narrower scope; a more expensive one may include unnecessary items. Ask the supplier to explain each difference.
