For most plants, that means software that works with compatible cameras, filters out noise, and raises usable alerts for people, vehicles, boundaries, attendance and camera health without replacing the whole setup.

A good system should help a supervisor notice what changed, not force them to watch hours of routine movement.

## Check readiness before you deploy on a live line

1.  Map the decisions each camera needs to support. A gate camera, a line camera and a yard camera do different jobs, so the expected alert should be named before any pilot starts. Done looks like a simple zone sheet where each view is tied to one action, such as after-hours entry review, vehicle entry logging or unsafe approach checking.

2.  Test the view, lighting and scene stability. Analytics can only work with what the camera can actually see, so check angle, glare, blur, backlight, occlusion and whether the subject fills enough of the frame. If processing design is still open, compare the [ai nvr versus analytics box](/insights/ai-nvr-vs-analytics-box-existing-ip-cameras) path as part of the audit. Done looks like sample clips from day, night and shift change, marked usable or not usable by camera.

3.  Define who receives each alert and what counts as a good result. A pilot fails when alerts arrive with no owner, no review rule and no clear response window. Done looks like named recipients, quiet hours, escalation rules and a short list of events that are worth testing on a live line.

## The best fit for loading bays and vehicle entry control

ANPR is the best fit for loading bays, gatehouses and yards where the main question is which vehicle came in, which one went out, and when. It suits sites that still rely on manual registers, radio calls or long footage searches to check vehicle movement. When the entry lane is defined, ANPR gives a cleaner answer than general surveillance analytics.

What stands out is the audit trail. Plate-based review is faster than scrubbing footage. It also helps when a supervisor needs to confirm a truck visit or match a disputed exit time. It can even check whether the right vehicle reached the right bay. The [conditions where ANPR works](/insights/anpr-number-plate-recognition-when-it-works) matter, though, because lane angle, plate visibility and night lighting decide most of the outcome.

The trade-off is scope. ANPR is excellent at gate-side identification. It does not replace broader site analytics for people, perimeters or shop-floor safety.

## The best fit for attendance tracking at shift changes

Face attendance is the better fit when the main need is workforce records at shift changes, not broad scene monitoring. It suits factories with fixed entry points, repeat staff movement and a clear need to reduce manual attendance handling. In that setting, it is often a cleaner answer than trying to turn general-purpose analytics into an attendance system.

What stands out is the narrowness of the job. A [face recognition attendance system on existing CCTV](/face-recognition-attendance-system) is built around entry events, identity matching and attendance records, so review is simpler than open-ended alerting across a whole site. It works best when people enter through defined views and faces are captured clearly.

The trade-off is control. This is not the right tool for watching all movement everywhere, and it depends on enrolment quality, entry discipline and suitable camera placement.

## The best fit for PPE checks on the shop floor

PPE detection is the best fit when safety checks around machine zones matter more than broad surveillance. It suits sites where supervisors need quick notice of missing required kit in defined areas, especially where line access, tool use or hazard proximity already have clear rules. In those cases, a focused safety model is more useful than a general motion or intrusion alert.

What stands out is relevance. Good [PPE kit detection on the cameras your site already has](/ppe-kit-detection) helps a team review likely safety exceptions instead of watching routine production footage. That keeps attention on the few moments that may need intervention, coaching or a closer check.

The trade-off is scene dependence. PPE analytics need stable views and clear visibility of the required gear. They also need sensible zone design. That makes them a supervisor aid in defined areas, not a catch-all safety system for every corner of a plant.
