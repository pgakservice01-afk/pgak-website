A CCTV quote that says “cable included” leaves a large part of the installation undefined. Two quotes can list the same cameras while allowing very different routes, protection, connections and testing.

Before comparing the final prices, ask each installer to describe the installed cable system. A working picture during a short demonstration does not answer whether the route suits the environment or whether every camera receives the power it needs under the proposed operating conditions.

## Make the cable schedule part of the quote

Ask for a schedule that links each camera identifier to its cable type, estimated route length, network or recorder connection and power source. Include patch leads and intermediate connections rather than measuring only the visible straight-line distance.

For an IP system, the network design and PoE arrangement affect the specification. An analogue system needs a signal path and power arrangement appropriate to its equipment. Neither “IP” nor “analogue” is enough detail to approve an installation.

The useful comparison is whether the proposed components meet their documented requirements. Do not accept a blanket claim that a particular cable category solves every distance, power or environmental problem. See the [IP and analogue comparison](/insights/ip-vs-analogue-cameras-india) if the system type is unclear.

## Identify exposed sections before installation

Walk the proposed routes with the installer. Mark sections exposed to weather, sunlight, moving vehicles, machinery, water collection or routine maintenance work. Ask how the cable jacket, containment, junction boxes and entries address those conditions.

Protection should follow the selected products' installation instructions and the site design. Conduit is one possible component; writing “conduit everywhere” does not specify suitable materials, joints, drainage or termination. Ask who will restore disturbed routes after another contractor works nearby.

Keep a record of concealed routes and accessible junctions. This makes later maintenance a traceable task rather than a search for undocumented connections above ceilings or inside crowded cabinets.

## Check power as a system

A switch with enough physical ports does not automatically have enough power for all connected equipment. Request the proposed port allocation and total power calculation, including the operating features that will be enabled.

Axis explains that a camera's typical and maximum consumption describe different conditions, and that cable losses matter to the power estimate. Its figures are product-specific, so use the actual equipment datasheets. [Read the manufacturer's power guidance](https://whitepapers.axis.com/en-us/typical-and-maximum-power-consumption-in-axis-cameras).

For your quote, record the camera model, required power mode, switch or injector model and remaining capacity. If infrared illumination or a heater is part of the design, include it in the assessment rather than testing only a convenient daytime configuration.

## Agree the handover evidence

Make acceptance practical and observable:

- Match camera labels to the cable schedule and recorder channels.
- Obtain cable test results appropriate to the installed system.
- Check each required camera mode and the proposed power configuration.
- Review recorded footage as well as the live view.
- Record faults, corrective work and the date of the successful retest.

A brief test cannot reproduce every future condition. Note what was tested, what remains to be checked during normal operation and who owns that review. Keep the results with the equipment inventory and support contacts.

## Diagnose interruptions before adding software

Repeated gaps can have several causes, including power, network configuration, equipment, cabling or recording settings. Do not replace a camera or an entire cable route solely because the video froze once. Ask the technician to isolate the affected path and record the evidence.

For an analytics project, a readable stream and a useful camera view are separate requirements. A repaired connection will not correct an obstructed view. Use the [camera compatibility checklist](/platform/compatibility) to prepare both kinds of information.

## Common questions

### Does every CCTV installation need CAT6 cable?

No. Specify cable and connectors for the proposed camera, network, power method, distance and environment. The installer should document why the selected system meets the equipment requirements.

### Does a picture on the monitor prove the cabling is sound?

No. A live picture is one check. Ask for the agreed cable test results, power checks, recording review and documentation of each installed run.
