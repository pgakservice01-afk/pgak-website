## A bus is not a small building

A school bus moves, shakes, heats up in summer and loses power when the engine stops. Parents want to know their children are safe. The school wants a record if something goes wrong. Cameras help with both, but only if they keep working on the road.

This guide covers how to find the rule that applies to you, what to check when you choose a system, and how to keep it working.

## Step 1: Find the rule for your state

Requirements differ. After a fatal school bus accident in Uttar Pradesh, CBSE guidelines issued in 2017 called for CCTV, GPS and speed governors on school buses. Uttar Pradesh later issued its own order for cameras in school vehicles. A 2022 report said Telangana expected cameras at the front and rear of the bus with a digital video recorder. Kerala made cameras mandatory for certain contract carriages from April 2025, with video recording and night visibility.

We could not find one national standard, and some of these sources are old. So do not rely on a list like this one. Ask the state transport department, the school board and the regional transport office for the current order. Write down the order number and what it says about camera positions, recording and storage. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways has also published a [specification document for CCTV](https://morth.nic.in/sites/default/files/Detailed_Specification_Document_for_CCTV.pdf), which is worth reading if your vehicle category falls under it.

## Step 2: Decide what the cameras need to see

Start with the questions you want the footage to answer:

- Who got on and off, and when?
- What happened in the aisle or at the door?
- Who was driving, and what did the road ahead show?
- What happened at the rear seats?

Typical positions are one camera facing down the aisle from the front, one at the door, one forward-facing and one covering the rear. Follow the state order first. Then check for blind spots: seat backs can hide children from a front camera.

## Step 3: Check the hardware for a moving vehicle

Home-grade cameras are not made for buses. Ask the supplier about:

1. **Power.** Buses run on vehicle power, which can dip and spike. Ask how the recorder handles engine start and how it shuts down safely.
2. **Vibration and heat.** The recorder should be fixed so it does not shake loose, and kept away from direct sun.
3. **Storage.** Ask what card or disk is used, how long it records at your video quality, and what happens when it is full. Our guide to [working out CCTV storage days](/insights/cctv-storage-how-many-days) explains the sum.
4. **Night view.** Early morning and late evening routes need clear low-light pictures.
5. **Clock.** Wrong time stamps weaken the footage. Ask how the clock is set and kept right.

## Step 4: Plan who reviews the footage

A camera that nobody checks gives little protection. Decide before installation:

- who may view the footage, and who may copy it,
- when it is reviewed, for instance only after a complaint or on a set schedule,
- how a copy is exported and logged,
- how long recordings are kept, and who deletes them.

Children are in the picture, so keep access narrow and keep a log. Our post on [who should be allowed to view CCTV footage at work](/insights/who-can-view-cctv-footage-at-work) gives a simple way to set roles, and the same thinking applies to a bus. Tell parents and staff that cameras are in use, and what they are used for.

## Step 5: Test it with a real route

Before accepting the system, run it for a full week. Check each day that:

- every camera recorded the whole trip,
- the time stamp is right,
- the pictures are usable at the door and in the aisle,
- the system started and stopped cleanly with the engine,
- a clip can be found and copied in a few minutes.

Write down any gap. A supplier who cannot explain a gap is a warning sign.

## Step 6: Keep checking after day one

Many school bus systems fail quietly. A cable loosens, a card wears out, or a camera is knocked sideways. Tribune reporting has noted that many school buses have non-functional cameras, though it does not tie the problem to one state. Build a simple routine:

1. The driver or attendant confirms the recorder light at the start of each trip.
2. A staff member checks one clip per bus each week.
3. A technician checks wiring, mounts and cards each term.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is CCTV compulsory in school buses in India?

It depends on the state and the type of vehicle. Some states have issued orders for cameras in school vehicles, and CBSE guidelines have also called for them. There is no single rule we could confirm for every state, so check the current order from your transport department.

### How many cameras does a school bus need?

It depends on the rule and the bus. A common layout covers the front door and the aisle, plus the rear. Some state orders name the positions, so use those first, then add cameras only where the view has a gap.

### What makes school bus CCTV stop working?

Vibration, heat, loose wiring, a failing memory card and cut power are common causes. A system that looks fine on the first day can fail silently later. Plan a regular check that confirms each camera is recording.
