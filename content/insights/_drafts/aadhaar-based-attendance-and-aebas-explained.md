**Straight answer: AEBAS — the Aadhaar Enabled Biometric Attendance System — is the Government of India's attendance platform, built and run by NIC for government organisations. Official documents describe it as a service for ministries, departments, PSUs, autonomous bodies and government institutions, not a product a private business can procure. If you run a private factory or shop, AEBAS is almost certainly not relevant to you; the useful question is which attendance system gives you a record you can defend on payroll day.**

Search "aadhaar attendance" and you get a government portal, a pile of biometric-machine listings and some vendors implying they can connect a factory to Aadhaar. Here is what the official sources actually say, checked on 8 October 2026.

## What do the official documents say AEBAS is?

| Document | Date | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| DoPT Office Memorandum No. 11013/9/2014-Estt (A-III), "Introduction of AADHAR Enabled Bio-metric Attendance System" | 21 November 2014 | AEBAS to be used "in all offices of the Central Government, including attached/sub-ordinate Offices". It calls the system "only an enabling platform": rules on office hours and late attendance do not change. |
| DoPT Office Memorandum No. 11013/9/2014-Estt.A-III, "Observance of punctuality in Government Offices" | 22 June 2015 | AEBAS to be implemented in all ministries and departments; employees to mark attendance on the portal regularly. |
| MeitY Office Memorandum No. 10(24)/2023-EG-II, forwarding the "AEBAS implementation Policy of Government of India", Version 1.1 | 16 November 2023 (policy dated October 2023) | Service "may be used by any kind of Government Organization/Schemes". Users mark attendance "through UIDAI registered devices by various method like finger-print, face authentication, Iris". Free for central ministries and departments, judicial and apex bodies and state government departments; paid, through NICSI, for other government bodies. The organisation signs an MoU with NICSI and appoints a nodal officer. NIC keeps attendance data for five years — two online, three archived. |
| attendance.gov.in portal | Checked 8 October 2026 | Shows organisation onboarding, a face-authentication app for personal devices, and public attendance dashboards. |

Two corrections to what is often repeated online, including in an earlier version of this article: AEBAS is **not fingerprint or iris only** — the 2023 policy and the portal both describe face authentication on registered devices; and it is **not limited to central government offices** — the policy extends it to state governments and other government bodies.

## Can a private employer get Aadhaar-based attendance?

The Aadhaar (Targeted Delivery of Financial and Other Subsidies, Benefits and Services) Act, 2016, as amended and published by UIDAI, sets the conditions:

- **Section 4(3):** an Aadhaar holder may *voluntarily* use their number for authentication; the explanation says voluntary use means use only with informed consent.
- **Section 4(4):** an entity "may be allowed to perform authentication" only if UIDAI is satisfied it meets prescribed privacy and security standards **and** it is either permitted to offer authentication under another law made by Parliament, or seeking authentication for a purpose the Central Government, in consultation with UIDAI and "in the interest of State", prescribes.
- **Section 4(6):** a requesting entity must tell the person about "alternate and viable means of identification" and must not deny a service for refusing or being unable to authenticate.
- **Section 57**, which once allowed use of Aadhaar under "any contract", was **omitted** by the Aadhaar and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019, with effect from 25 July 2019, following the Supreme Court's 2018 Aadhaar judgment. <!-- UNRESOLVED: judgment not read from an official source; mentioned as background only. -->

**What changed in 2025.** The Aadhaar Authentication for Good Governance (Social Welfare, Innovation, Knowledge) Amendment Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 88(E), 31 January 2025) let an entity other than a government ministry or department prepare a proposal, "for a purpose specified in rule 3 and in the interest of State", and submit it to the concerned ministry. If the ministry agrees, it forwards the proposal; if UIDAI is satisfied, the Central Government may authorise the ministry to notify it. The amendment added "promoting ease of living of residents and enabling better access to services for them" to the purposes. <!-- UNRESOLVED: full rule 3 purposes list not read. -->

So the honest answer has changed from "the door is shut" to "there is a formal route, and we found no notification that uses it for private-sector staff attendance". A vendor who says they can "arrange Aadhaar attendance" for your factory should be asked for the specific notification. The Act also contains penalties, including for unauthorised access to the Central Identities Data Repository (section 38) and unauthorised use by a requesting entity (section 40).

**Storing Aadhaar numbers is a different thing.** Keeping an employee's Aadhaar number as a text field is not authentication and gives no attendance benefit — matching is still done by your own fingerprint or face system. It does add another piece of personal data to protect. Your own employee code does the same job.

## AEBAS and private face attendance, side by side (the asset)

| | AEBAS | A private face-attendance system (for example, on a factory's own entrance cameras) |
|---|---|---|
| Who runs it | NIC, with NICSI as implementing agency | The employer, with its supplier |
| Who may use it | Government organisations and schemes (AEBAS policy, Nov 2023) | Any employer that sets it up |
| Whose biometric data is matched | UIDAI authentication through registered devices | The employer's own enrolment templates; no UIDAI connection |
| Where data is kept | NIC; five years (policy) | Where the employer and supplier decide; retention set by the employer |
| Legal framework named | Aadhaar Act, IT Act and other law (policy, section 9) | The employer's own duties, including the DPDP Act, 2023 from May 2027 |
| Typical evidence of a mark | AEBAS record | Depends on the system — some keep the frame that produced each mark |
| What you buy | Devices and, where applicable, NICSI service | Software, licences or hardware, as quoted per site |

## What PGAK is not claiming

So there is no confusion with search results that mix the two:

- PGAK does **not** claim to be an AEBAS provider, NIC partner or UIDAI-approved entity, and its attendance offer does not involve Aadhaar authentication.
- PGAK's face attendance does **not** need an Aadhaar number and does not connect to UIDAI. Any templates are the employer's own enrolment.
- PGAK does **not** claim that face attendance is "Aadhaar-verified" or "government-approved".
- PGAK has **no published accuracy figure** for face attendance. Whether it works at your gates depends on camera position, light and stream quality, and is assessed per site.

## Limitations

This article is for buyers trying to tell AEBAS apart from private systems. If you work in a government office, your department's own orders and the AEBAS portal are the authority, not this page.
