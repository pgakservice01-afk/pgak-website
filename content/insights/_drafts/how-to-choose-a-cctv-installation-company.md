**Straight answer: choose a CCTV installation company in Ludhiana on three things you can check before you pay — whether they survey your site properly, whether your address is genuinely inside the area they service, and whether "commissioning" is defined in writing. The per-camera price is the least useful number on the quote, because the problems that cost you money arrive in month six, and none of them appear on the quotation.**

Ludhiana has no shortage of installers. Search for one and you will find shop-front dealers, technicians who work on their own, and companies that design larger systems. Any of them can be the right choice. What separates them is not their size but how they handle the dull parts: the survey, the scope, the handover and the phone call after a camera goes dark.

## Why the survey tells you more than the quote

A quote is only as good as the walk-round behind it. An installer who prices your factory from a phone call, or from a count of "how many cameras do you want", has priced a guess.

Ludhiana sites have particular conditions that a good survey looks for. Hosiery and textile units carry fibre and lint in the air, which settles on lenses and inside housings. Cycle-parts, forging and auto-component units have metal dust, oil mist, heat and vibration from presses. Many compounds have long boundary walls with poor night lighting, open plots next door, and power cuts that a recorder without a UPS does not survive cleanly. Every one of these changes the camera, housing, mounting or power design — and none of them can be seen from a desk.

### What a proper survey visit should cover (checklist)

Use this to judge any installer's survey. If they skip most of it, their quote is a guess.

- [ ] They walked the whole site, including the boundary, dispatch area and recorder location — not just the office and the main gate.
- [ ] They asked what each camera must **show**: a face at the gate, a number plate, a person crossing the rear wall at night, or just general activity.
- [ ] They looked at the site **after dark**, or asked for a phone video of it, and noted where there is no usable light.
- [ ] They noted dust, oil, heat, rain exposure and vibration at each outdoor or production-floor position.
- [ ] They checked where power will come from at each camera position, and whether the recorder and network switch need a UPS.
- [ ] They measured or paced the cable runs instead of estimating a round figure.
- [ ] They asked how many days of footage you need, and said how they will size storage to reach it.
- [ ] They checked whether existing cameras and the recorder can be reused, and said how they tested that.
- [ ] They asked who in your team will receive alerts or view footage, and on which phones.
- [ ] They noted what needs a ladder, a lift or roof access, and who will provide it.
- [ ] They gave you a written camera schedule: position, purpose, camera type, and what it will not cover.

If you are planning a new or rebuilt factory, the camera count itself is better worked out from the events you care about than from floor area; our guide on [how many CCTV cameras a factory needs](/insights/how-many-cctv-cameras-does-a-factory-need) walks through that method.

## Check the installer's actual service area

A city name on a website, a visiting card or a Google listing is not the same thing as a technician who can reach your gate. This matters more in Ludhiana than it first appears, because industrial units sit in several belts across and around the city, and an installer who is ten minutes from one may be a long drive from another.

Ask these and write the answers down:

| Question | Good answer | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Who will do the survey, and who will install? | Named people, their own staff or a named subcontractor | "Our team" with no names, or the installer is never the person you met |
| Where does that technician start their day? | A specific location you can judge | Vague: "we cover all of Punjab" |
| Is my site inside your normal service area? | Yes, with similar sites nearby | "We can manage" |
| What is the response time to a dead camera at **my** address, in hours? | A number, in writing | "Same day", "immediately", "always available" |
| What happens on a Sunday or a holiday? | A defined arrangement, even if it costs extra | No answer |
| Can I phone two existing customers near me? | Yes | Only photographs of fresh installations |

A written forty-eight-hour commitment is worth more than a spoken promise of same-day service. And a reference customer who has had the system for more than a year tells you more than any portfolio, because anyone can hand over a working system on day one.

## Ask for a transparent commissioning scope

"Installation and commissioning" is often one line on a quote with one number beside it. Ask for it to be broken out. A transparent scope lists what will be true on the day you sign the handover:

- Every camera recording at the resolution and frame rate written in the quote.
- Retention checked on the recorder itself, in days, at those settings — not calculated on paper. Our explainer on [how many days of footage you are really keeping](/insights/cctv-storage-how-many-days) shows why the two differ.
- Recorder and camera clocks synchronised, so timestamps can be trusted later.
- Remote viewing working on the owner's own phone, tested in front of the owner.
- The recorder admin password, and every camera password, handed to the owner in writing. Default passwords changed.
- A camera list showing each camera's location, model, firmware version and IP address, plus a simple cable-route drawing.
- Video streams left accessible in a standard way (ONVIF or RTSP) for authorised software you may add later, with the settings documented. Our [ONVIF integration guide](/features/guides/onvif-integration) explains why this keeps your options open.
- Warranty registered in the owner's name, with invoices that list each item.
- A short training session: how to search, play back and export a clip.

If an installer will not commit to most of these in writing, you will be paying them again later to do the parts they skipped.

## The questions that still matter after the survey

The earlier version of this guide set out seven questions. They still hold, and a good Ludhiana installer will answer all of them without hesitation:

1. **Who owns the password?** You should, from day one, in writing. An installer who keeps the admin credentials controls your footage.
2. **What does a dead camera cost you in time?** Get the response in hours.
3. **Are cabling and power itemised?** Cable runs, conduit, mounting at height and power to the far end of a compound are where real cost and corner-cutting live. Our note on [where cabling corners get cut](/insights/cctv-cabling-corner-cutting) lists what to look for.
4. **What happens when the disk fills?** Every recorder overwrites eventually; make the installer size storage to the retention you need.
5. **Can I see a system you installed more than a year ago?** A live view today, not a photograph.
6. **What exactly does the "smart" feature do?** If you are sold "AI" or "smart detection", ask which events raise an alert, who receives it, and how it avoids firing for animals, headlights and swaying trees.
7. **Who tunes it after handover?** Detection zones, schedules and alert recipients need adjusting against real activity, or the alerts get muted within weeks.

## Reuse or replace: watch for the conflict of interest

An installer who earns from selling cameras has a reason to say your existing ones are obsolete. Sometimes they are. Often a well-placed older camera with a usable stream is fine, and the money is better spent on the gate or the boundary. Ask the installer to show you, camera by camera, why each one fails. ## When a bigger installer, or no new installer, is the better choice

A small local technician is often the fastest to reach a dead camera, but may not be set up for a multi-building factory with networked recorders, long fibre runs and several user accounts. A larger company may be stronger on design and paperwork but slower to send someone to a single site. Neither is automatically right; the survey and the written scope will tell you which you are dealing with.

And sometimes the right answer is not a new installation at all. If your cameras work and the problem is that nobody maintains them, a properly written maintenance contract may fix more than a new system would. Our guide to [what a CCTV AMC should include](/insights/cctv-amc-what-should-it-include) covers that.

## The short version

Judge the survey, check the service area for your actual address, and get commissioning defined in writing. Then ask the seven questions. The field usually narrows itself.
