**Straight answer: remote CCTV monitoring lets an owner or manager see several sites from one phone or office, and it can save the site visits whose only purpose was to look — whether the shutter is down, whether the night shift started, whether a delivery was unloaded. It cannot save the visits where someone has to be there: to sign, collect, inspect by hand, fix, talk to staff or respond to an incident. Keep a travel diary for a month, count only the looking visits a camera could genuinely have replaced, and price those at your real cost per trip.**

Remote viewing replaces looking, not attending. Every honest calculation of its value starts from that sentence, and most sales pitches skip it.

## What is the difference between looking and attending?

| Looking — a camera may replace the trip | Attending — someone still has to go |
|---|---|
| Confirming shutters and gates are closed | Collecting cash or documents |
| Checking that a shift has started or a gate is staffed | Signing, approving or handing over |
| Seeing whether a delivery was unloaded | Quality inspection that needs touching or measuring |
| Glancing at housekeeping or stacking in a visible area | Machinery breakdowns and repairs |
| Checking that a contractor arrived | Meeting staff, training, resolving disputes |
| Checking a site after a storm or a power cut, where the cameras still work | Responding to an alarm, an injury or a break-in |

A camera also has to be able to show the thing. A ceiling camera cannot tell you the moisture in a grain stack or whether a meter reads correctly; a gate camera at night may not show whether a padlock is closed.

## What has to be in place first?

- **Each site's recorder reachable remotely**, through the recorder's own app or a platform, with an account per person — not one shared password passed around for years.
- **Enough upload bandwidth at each site** for live view of the cameras you will actually check. A site on a weak connection may manage one camera at a time.
- **Cameras that show what you check.** Walk through your usual reasons for visiting and confirm which camera answers each.
- **A local response contact** for every site — someone who can go and open, close, check or call the police when remote viewing shows a problem.
- **A way to know a site has gone dark.** If the link or the recorder fails, remote viewing shows nothing, and silence is easy to mistake for calm. The [camera offline article](/insights/cctv-camera-offline-how-to-know) and [does AI CCTV work without internet](/insights/does-ai-cctv-work-without-internet) cover what fails and how you would find out.

## The travel diary

Keep it for one ordinary month — not a festival month, not the month of an audit. Everyone who travels between sites fills in one line per visit, on the day.

| Date | Site | Who travelled | Purpose (in their words) | Look or attend? | Could a camera have shown what was needed? (which one) | Would someone still have had to go? | Round-trip km | Cash cost of trip (₹) | Travel hours | Avoidable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Oct | Site B | Ops manager | Check night shift started | Look | Yes — Gate-B | No | | | | Yes |
| 3 Oct | Site C | Ops manager | Collect dispatch documents, check bay | Attend + look | Partly | Yes | | | | No |
| | | | | | | | | | | |

### Rules for the last column

Mark a visit **avoidable only if all three are true**: its purpose was looking; a specific camera could have shown what was needed; and nobody would have had to go anyway. Be strict:

- **Mixed visits count as not avoidable.** If the person still had to collect documents, the trip still happens.
- **Visits that found a problem count as not avoidable** if the problem needed someone on site. Remote viewing would have found it sooner, but someone would still have gone.
- **Visits whose real purpose was to be seen** — a manager's presence on the floor — are a management choice, not a viewing task. Leave them out of the saving.

### Pricing a trip

Use your own figures: your vehicle running cost per kilometre or your fuel and maintenance records, tolls, driver cost if there is one, and any allowance paid. Keep travel hours in a separate column; they are time, not cash.

## Worked example

**Illustrative example** (hypothetical figures): an owner with three sites keeps the diary for a month.

- 24 visits recorded.
- 9 had looking as their only purpose, and in 8 of those a specific camera could have shown what was needed.
- In 1 of those 8, the person found a problem that needed someone on site anyway.
- Avoidable visits = 7. Replaceable share = 7 ÷ 24 ≈ 0.29.
- Cash cost per trip, from their own records: ₹1,000. Travel time: 3 hours per round trip.
- Checking the sites remotely instead, plus routine remote checks, adds about 4 hours a month.

cash avoided = visits × replaceable share × cash per visit = 24 × (7 ÷ 24) × ₹1,000 = ₹7,000 a month

hours released = visits × replaceable share × travel hours − remote review hours = 24 × (7 ÷ 24) × 3 − 4 = 21 − 4 = 17 hours a month

Then set the recurring cost of remote access against the cash — connectivity at each site, any platform subscription, any equipment needed to make the recorders reachable. Those costs come from your own quotes. If ₹7,000 a month of avoided travel does not cover them, the case rests on the 17 hours, and hours are capacity: they become cash only if overtime stops, a post goes unfilled or a vehicle is no longer needed.

Your diary might just as easily show two avoidable visits out of 24. That is worth knowing before you pay for anything.

## What remote monitoring does not do

- **It does not respond.** When something is wrong at 2 am at an empty site, remote viewing tells you; a local person has to go. Do not reduce guarding, lighting or local supervision on the strength of a remote view.
- **It does not watch by itself.** Viewing on demand depends on someone remembering to look. Alerts that come to a named person are a different design; the [remote CCTV monitoring page](/remote-cctv-monitoring) covers that distinction.
- **It does not work without a link.** Plan for outages at each site, not just the head office.

## Keeping remote access safe

- One account per person, with only the sites and cameras they need; remove it the day someone leaves.
- Use two-step login where the recorder or platform offers it.
- Never share recorder passwords, stream addresses or footage on WhatsApp or by email.
- Keep recorder firmware updated, and be wary of exposing a recorder directly to the internet without understanding the risk; ask whoever maintains your system how remote access is set up.
- Remote viewing of staff areas raises the same privacy questions as viewing in the office — see the [workplace privacy article](/insights/cctv-workplace-privacy-india).

## When it does not pay

- Sites are close together and visits are mostly attending.
- Upload bandwidth at the sites cannot carry a usable live view.
- The diary shows few avoidable visits — which is a perfectly good reason not to buy.
