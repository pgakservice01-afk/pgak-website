**Straight answer: there is no honest single price for AI CCTV in India, because suppliers publish prices for different things — a software subscription, a camera system, a complete kit. To compare quotes, split every one into the same five lines: processing hardware, software licence, installation and commissioning, support, and tax. The cheapest headline figure is often the one that has moved a line somewhere you cannot see.**

A buyer collecting three quotes usually ends up with three documents that cannot be laid side by side. One is a monthly figure per camera. One is a lump sum for cameras and a recorder. One is a single number for "the system". The worksheets below make them comparable.

These are not one product at five prices. A subscription you run on your own computer, a camera system priced in lakhs and a quoted project with a survey are three different purchases. Before comparing numbers, decide which of the three you are buying. The [supplier comparison guide](/insights/compare-ai-video-analytics-suppliers-india) covers pilots, proof and the questions that separate suppliers beyond price.

## Which five lines should every quote show?

**1. Processing hardware.** Detection has to run somewhere: on the camera, on a computer or processing unit at your site, or on a remote server. At PGAK it runs on an on-site processing unit. Ask what device is supplied, how many camera streams it is sized for at your stream settings, who owns it, and who replaces it if it fails. A quote that says "no hardware needed" still needs a computer somewhere — find out whose.

**2. Software licence.** Ask for the basis (per camera, per site, per function or a one-time licence), the term, what is included in renewal, and what happens to the configuration if you stop paying.

**3. Installation and commissioning.** Stream access checks, configuration of zones and schedules, alert routing, and the tuning period after go-live. This line is often missing from cheap quotes; the work then falls to you.

**4. Support.** Hours, response time versus resolution time, what is excluded, and who visits site. A support line with no named terms is not a support line.

**5. Tax.** Ask for GST shown per line, not as a single total. It is the only way to check that the pre-tax figures match.

Two further lines appear on some quotes and not others: **new or repositioned cameras** (each one should have a reason — see [how to check which existing cameras can be added](/insights/add-ai-to-existing-cctv-cameras)) and **network or power work** such as a switch, cabling or a UPS.

## Three camera-count worksheets

Below are three typical site sizes. PGAK prices are **quoted per site**, so the PGAK column cannot be filled in from a website, and nor can most suppliers'. Use the worksheet to make every quote you receive answer the same questions. Copy it, and fill in one column per supplier.

### Worksheet A — small unit, 4 cameras

| Line | What to get in writing | Supplier 1 | Supplier 2 | PGAK |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Processing hardware | Device, capacity in streams, owner, replacement terms | ₹ | ₹ | Quoted per site |
| Licence | Basis, term, renewal, cameras covered | ₹ | ₹ | Quoted per site |
| Installation and commissioning | Stream checks, zones, alert routing, tuning period | ₹ | ₹ | Quoted per site |
| Support (per year) | Hours, response vs resolution, site visits | ₹ | ₹ | Quoted per site |
| New cameras, if any | Which camera, why, model | ₹ | ₹ | Itemised if needed |
| GST | Shown per line | ₹ | ₹ | Shown in quote |
| **Year-one total** | Sum of the above | ₹ | ₹ | — |

At this size a minimum charge often matters more than the per-camera rate. Foreman, for example, publishes a four-camera minimum.

### Worksheet B — mid-size factory, 16 cameras

Same lines as Worksheet A, plus:

| Extra line | Why it matters at this size |
|---|---|
| Processing capacity | Sixteen streams may need a larger device than four; ask for the stream count it is sized for |
| Camera-by-camera reuse list | Which cameras are reused, repositioned or replaced, with one reason each |
| Network | Whether the existing switch and recorder can serve the extra streams |

As a worked illustration of how a published per-camera rate multiplies: 16 cameras × ₹400 = ₹6,400 a month before GST, which is the figure Foreman itself publishes for a 16-camera unit. That covers its software. The computer it runs on, any network work and your own staff time are separate lines — not a criticism, just what the price is for.

### Worksheet C — multi-building site, 64 cameras

Same lines again, plus:

| Extra line | Why it matters at this size |
|---|---|
| Number of processing devices | Large sites may need more than one, or one per building |
| Alert routing | Who receives which alerts, at what hours, and what the escalation is |
| Phased rollout | Whether the licence starts per camera as each phase goes live, or for all cameras at once |
| Exit terms | What you keep — hardware, configuration, recorded events — if the contract ends |

## What makes quotes look cheaper or dearer than they are?

**Cheaper than it is:** the processing computer is yours to supply; tuning is not included; support is email only; the minimum term is long; GST is not shown.

**Dearer than it is:** a full camera replacement nobody has justified camera by camera; functions you did not ask for bundled in; cloud processing whose bandwidth you also pay for (use the [bandwidth and cloud cost calculator](/calculators/bandwidth-and-cloud-cost) to put a number on that line).

The quickest test is one question to each supplier: *which of my existing cameras will you use, what exactly is in each line, and who adjusts the system in the second week?* Vague answers to any of the three mean the quote is incomplete.

## When should you not buy AI CCTV yet?

If cameras do not cover the area that matters, if the scene is dark at night, or if nobody will receive and act on an alert, analytics will not fix the problem. Coverage, lighting and a response plan come first, and they are cheaper.
