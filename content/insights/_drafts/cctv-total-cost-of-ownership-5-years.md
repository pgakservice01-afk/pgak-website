**Straight answer: five-year CCTV cost = capital + replacements + electricity + subscriptions + maintenance + downtime. The installation quote covers only the first line. On the illustrative example below, that first line comes to a little over half of the five-year total — the rest is spent quietly, year by year, on things the quote never mentions. Fill in the worksheet with your own figures before comparing suppliers who price differently.**

Suppliers structure quotes in different shapes: one sells hardware for a lump sum, another adds a monthly charge, a third bundles both. A five-year view is the only fair way to compare them, and it also prevents the unplanned capital request in year three when a recorder fails.

## The six lines, and what goes in each

| Line | What to include | Where the figure comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Capital** | Cameras, recorder, disks, cabling, installation, any processing unit | The quotes |
| **2. Replacement** | Cameras, disks or devices you expect to replace in five years | Your own failure history, or a stated assumption |
| **3. Energy** | Everything that runs 24 hours: cameras, recorder, processing unit, switch, router | Device labels × hours × your tariff |
| **4. Subscription** | Software licences, cloud storage, remote-access or monitoring services | The quotes, × 60 months |
| **5. Maintenance** | AMC, cleaning, re-aiming, firmware updates | AMC quote or last year's spend |
| **6. Downtime** | Call-out visits, and a note of days without coverage | Your records |

Downtime has a cash part (visits, rush repairs) and a non-cash part (an incident nobody recorded). Put the cash part in the total and list the non-cash part next to it; do not invent a rupee value for footage you did not have.

## Worked five-year cost: an illustrative example

**All values are hypothetical round inputs, not market or PGAK prices.** The site is a plain CCTV system with no analytics.

| Line | Arithmetic | Five-year cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Capital | 16 × ₹5,000 + ₹30,000 + ₹20,000 + ₹40,000 | ₹1,70,000 |
| 2. Replacement — cameras | 8 outdoor ÷ 4 = 2 × ₹5,000 | ₹10,000 |
| 2. Replacement — disk | 1 × ₹10,000 | ₹10,000 |
| 2. Storage added | | ₹10,000 |
| 3. Energy | (16 × 5 W + 40 W) = 120 W; 120 × 24 × 365 ÷ 1,000 = 1,051.2 kWh a year; × ₹8 = ₹8,409.60 a year; × 5 | ₹42,048 |
| 4. Subscription | none | ₹0 |
| 5. Maintenance | 4 years × ₹15,000 | ₹60,000 |
| 6. Downtime (cash) | 2 × ₹1,000 × 5 years | ₹10,000 |
| **Five-year total** | | **₹3,12,048** |

On these inputs, the capital quote (₹1,70,000) is about 54% of the five-year total (₹1,70,000 ÷ ₹3,12,048). The other ₹1,42,048 is spread across five years and never appears on the invoice you signed.

## Adding an analytics option to the worksheet

If you are comparing a plain system with one that adds analytics, add these lines from the analytics quote rather than guessing them:

| Extra line | Formula | Illustrative figure |
|---|---|---|
| Processing unit capital | From the quote | Quoted per site |
| Licence and support | Monthly charge × 60 | Quoted per site |
| Processing unit energy | Watts × 24 × 365 × 5 ÷ 1,000 × tariff | A hypothetical 60 W unit: 60 × 24 × 365 ÷ 1,000 = 525.6 kWh a year × ₹8 = ₹4,204.80; × 5 = ₹21,024 |
| Processing unit replacement | Who pays if it fails, and the cost | From the quote's terms |

## Why do costs creep up after installation?

**Storage.** Retention is sized for today's cameras. Add four cameras and the same disks hold fewer days; replace a camera with a higher-resolution one and the files grow. See [how many days of CCTV footage you are really keeping](/insights/cctv-storage-how-many-days).

**Outdoor wear.** Heat, dust and monsoon humidity are hard on housings, connectors and infrared. Keep a record of every failure; after a year it is a better basis for line 2 than any rule of thumb.

**Unsupported recorders.** A recorder that stops receiving firmware updates may still record, but becomes harder to integrate and secure.

**Unnoticed failures.** A camera that dies quietly costs nothing until the day footage is needed. Line 6 exists to make that visible. See [how to know a CCTV camera is offline](/insights/cctv-camera-offline-how-to-know) and [what a CCTV AMC should include](/insights/cctv-amc-what-should-it-include).

## Limitations

The worksheet is only as good as its assumptions. Failure rates and AMC costs vary by site and supplier; use your own records where you have them, and state the rest as assumptions. For a three- or four-camera shop the extra lines are small enough that an annual repair budget may be simpler than a formal model. And the total says nothing about whether the system is worth having — only what it costs.
