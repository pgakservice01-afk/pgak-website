**Straight answer: a CCTV annual maintenance contract should name every covered device and commit, in writing, to support hours, a response time and a resolution time for each severity, scheduled preventive visits, a parts policy, a list of exclusions, and proof at every visit that each camera is recording. If AI analytics runs on the system, the contract, or a separate support agreement, must also say who looks after the processing unit and who replaces it when it fails. Anything vaguer is a payment plan, not maintenance.**

The usual AMC is a fee and a line saying the vendor will attend when called. That leaves the important questions unanswered: how quickly, what counts as fixed, which parts are included and how you would know the system was recording in the first place.

## What makes an AMC real?

Seven things separate a maintenance contract from an invoice:

1. **Response and resolution times in hours,** by severity, in writing, with what happens if they are missed.
2. **Scheduled preventive visits** on dates, whether or not anything is broken. This is the clause dropped first to meet a price.
3. **Physical cleaning and inspection:** lenses, housings, seals, brackets, cable joints, earthing.
4. **Recording verification:** proof, not a tick, that each camera records and that retention matches what you think you have.
5. **Firmware and access hygiene:** updates from the manufacturer only, default passwords removed, a record of who holds admin access.
6. **A parts policy:** what is included, what is charged, what is lent during repair, and lead times.
7. **A written report after every visit** that you can check against the system.

## A service-scope template you can fill in

Use this as a schedule attached to the contract. The example wording is **illustrative**. Set the numbers to what your site needs and what the provider will actually commit to.

| Clause | What to write | Example wording (illustrative) | Evidence you receive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Covered assets | Every camera, recorder, disk, switch, power supply and UPS, by ID, location and serial | "Schedule A lists 24 cameras, 1 NVR, 2 disks, 2 PoE switches, 1 UPS." | Asset register, updated after every change |
| Support hours | When faults can be logged and when work happens | "Faults logged 24×7 by phone or email; attended Monday–Saturday, 09:00–19:00." | Ticket number for every call |
| Severity levels | What counts as critical | "Severity 1: recorder down, all recording stopped, or a camera named in Schedule B down. Severity 2: any other single camera. Severity 3: image or cosmetic faults." | Severity recorded on each ticket |
| Response time | Acknowledge and start diagnosis | "Severity 1: 2 working hours. Severity 2: 8 working hours." | Ticket timestamps |
| Resolution time | Working and recording again, or a temporary fix in place | "Severity 1: restored within 24 hours or a loan unit fitted. Severity 2: within 72 hours." | Playback screenshot after the fix |
| Clock start and stop | Remove arguments later | "Clock starts when the ticket is logged and stops when recording is confirmed by playback." | Same |
| Preventive visits | Frequency and checklist | "Quarterly, on dates agreed in advance, using Checklist C." | Signed checklist per visit |
| Recording-check evidence | Proof each camera records | "Each visit: per camera, a playback check of footage from the previous day and the oldest available day, with timestamps; retention days and disk health read from the recorder; recorder clock checked against network time." | Screenshot or log per camera |
| Parts | Included or chargeable | "Labour included. Parts chargeable at quoted rates, except connectors, glands and consumables under ₹___ each." | Parts used, listed per visit |
| Standby and lead time | Cover while waiting | "A loan camera and power supply fitted within the resolution time where a part is not in stock." | Loan record |
| Exclusions | Written, so there are no surprises | "Physical damage, theft, lightning or surge, rodent damage, civil work, cabling beyond 10 m of a device, changes by third parties." | — |
| Firmware and access | Who changes what | "Firmware updated only from the manufacturer, with the version recorded. Admin credentials held by the client; no sharing of passwords over messaging apps." | Firmware log; access log |
| Reporting | Timing and content | "Written report within 3 working days of each visit." | The report |
| If targets are missed | A remedy you can use | "Each missed Severity 1 resolution extends the contract by one month at no charge." | — |
| Exit | Clean handover | "On termination: configuration backup, passwords, asset register and as-built drawings handed over." | Handover record |

Two clauses deserve more attention than they usually get.

**Recording-check evidence.** A camera can show a live picture while its disk has failed. Asking for a playback check of the oldest available footage on each camera, with the retention read off the recorder, turns "visited" into "verified". The [camera offline guide](/insights/cctv-camera-offline-how-to-know) includes a failure-injection test you can ask the provider to run with you once a year.

**Exclusions.** Every contract has them. The ones that cause disputes are those nobody wrote down. Lightning, rodents and civil work are common causes of outdoor faults, so agree in advance how those repairs will be quoted.

## What extra terms do you need once AI analytics is involved?

Analytics adds a layer the camera installer's AMC usually does not cover: the processing unit, the software and the detection rules. Whether these sit in one contract or in a separate support agreement with the analytics supplier, get the following in writing.

**Support hours for the analytics layer.** They may differ from camera support hours. If alerts matter at night, ask what happens at night.

**Response versus resolution, defined for analytics.** *Response* means someone acknowledges the fault and starts diagnosing it, usually remotely. *Resolution* means alerts are flowing again on the affected cameras. Write both down, by severity. "Alerts stopped across the site" is not the same severity as "one rule needs retuning".

**Who replaces failed processing hardware.** This is the clause most often missing. The agreement should answer:

- Who diagnoses whether the fault is the processing unit, the network, the recorder or a camera?
- Who supplies the replacement unit, and is it covered by warranty, by the support fee or charged separately?
- Is a standby unit kept, and how long does replacement take?
- Who reinstalls the software and restores the configuration — camera list, zones, rules, schedules — and is a configuration backup kept?
- What happens to event data stored on a failed unit?

**Changes made by others.** If the CCTV installer replaces a camera, changes its angle or updates the recorder's firmware, analytics zones and streams may stop working. Agree who tells whom, and who re-checks the analytics afterwards.

**Software updates.** Who installs them, who tests detection afterwards, and whether an update can be rolled back.

**Remote access.** How the supplier connects for support, with your approval, through which method, and logged. Credentials are never shared over WhatsApp or email.

**Health reporting.** What evidence the supplier gives you each month that cameras, streams and the processing unit are working.

## What should you check before renewing?

1. Ask for the last twelve months of visit reports. If they do not exist, that tells you more than any quote comparison.
2. Check three things yourself today: how many cameras are offline, how many days of footage you really hold (play back the oldest), and who has the recorder's admin password.
3. Put the template above in front of your current provider and ask them to fill in the numbers. Their willingness to commit is the most useful thing you will learn.

## Where an AMC is not the answer

A small site with a handful of cameras may do better with a reliable local technician on call plus its own monthly playback check, rather than a fixed annual fee. An AMC also cannot rescue a system that was badly installed. If cabling, earthing or camera choice is wrong, that is a one-time correction before any contract makes sense.
