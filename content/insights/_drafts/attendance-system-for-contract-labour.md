**Straight answer: contract labour is where attendance records most often break, because enrolment takes longer than many contract workers stay. The fix is a weekly reconciliation between the contractor's roster and your own gate record, with every difference reviewed by a named person and answered by the contractor. Under the OSH Code, the principal employer can end up liable for unpaid contract wages, so an independent record is a compliance matter as well as a commercial one.**

On many industrial sites, permanent staff are on an attendance system and contract labour is on a register kept by the contractor. The party with the least reason to keep that register accurate is the one keeping it.

## What does the law put on the principal employer?

India's four labour codes came into force on 21 November 2025, according to the Ministry of Labour and Employment's *FAQs on Labour Codes* (labour.gov.in, January 2026). The same FAQ says that during the transition, old rules remain in force until new rules are notified under the Codes, to the extent they are in line with the Codes.

For contract labour, the relevant text is Chapter XI, Part I of the **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020** (No. 37 of 2020, Gazette of India, 29 September 2020):

- **Section 45(1)** applies that Part to every establishment in which fifty or more contract labour are, or were on any day in the preceding twelve months, employed through contract, and to manpower supply contractors with fifty or more.
- **Section 53** requires the principal employer to provide the welfare facilities in sections 23 and 24 to contract labour. MoLE's *FAQ on OSH&WC Code, 2020* (as on 13 March 2026) restates this.
- **Section 55(1)–(2)** makes the contractor responsible for paying wages within the prescribed period, by bank transfer or electronic mode, and requires the contractor to inform the principal employer electronically of the amount paid.
- **Section 55(3)** makes the principal employer liable to pay the wages in full, or the unpaid balance, if the contractor fails to pay on time or short-pays, and lets the principal employer recover that amount from the contractor.
- **Section 33** requires an employer to maintain registers, electronically or otherwise, including attendance, overtime and wages, in the form prescribed by the appropriate government.

What is clear from section 55 is that if the contractor's pay records are wrong, the risk can come back to you, so you need your own view of who worked.

## Why does contract attendance break?

**Enrolment costs more than tenure justifies.** If enrolling one worker needs an office visit, a supervisor and several fingerprint captures, a gang on a two-week job will not be enrolled. The register absorbs them.

**Gangs change daily.** The contractor sends whoever is available. The names on Monday's list are not always the faces at Tuesday's gate.

**Shifts and sites blur.** One contractor may supply three sites, moving workers between them. Without a shift and site on every line, a worker-day can be billed twice.

**Nobody owns the comparison.** Accounts pays the invoice; HR keeps permanent attendance; security sees the gate. No one compares the three.

## The anonymised contractor roster

Ask each contractor to send a roster before the week starts, in this format. It uses a worker reference instead of a name so it can circulate between accounts and the gate without spreading personal details. The contractor keeps the key that links references to names and identity documents.

| Worker ref | Contractor | Work order | Site | Shift | Planned days (Mon–Sat) | Enrolled at gate? | Induction done? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1-014 | Contractor 1 | WO-0815 | Plant A | A (06:00–14:00) | 6 | Yes | Yes |
| C1-015 | Contractor 1 | WO-0815 | Plant A | A (06:00–14:00) | 6 | No — new | Pending |
| C2-003 | Contractor 2 | WO-0822 | Plant A | C (22:00–06:00) | 5 | Yes | Yes |

Rules for the roster: one row per worker per week; a substitute gets a new reference, not someone else's; no worker is scheduled on two sites or two overlapping shifts; any change after Monday is sent before the shift, not after.

### Illustrative weekly reconciliation

Illustrative example, round hypothetical numbers:

- Contractor 1 rostered 30 workers × 6 days = **180 worker-days**.
- The gate record matched 170 worker-days to rostered references.
- 6 gate entries were people not on the roster (substitutes or visitors).
- 4 rostered worker-days had no gate record at all.
- The invoice claims 180 worker-days.

Difference to explain: 180 claimed − 170 matched = **10 worker-days**, made up of 6 unrostered entries and 4 missing records. None of these is proof of anything yet. Each goes into the exception workflow below.

## The exception approval workflow

| Step | Who | What happens | By when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Flag | Attendance system or gate clerk | Each difference between roster and gate record is listed, with time, gate and any snapshot | End of each shift |
| 2. First review | Named site reviewer | Checks obvious causes: substitute not yet added, wrong shift, second gate, enrolment miss | Next working day |
| 3. Contractor response | Contractor's supervisor | Explains each remaining item with evidence: substitute's details, work order, supervisor sign-off | Within an agreed number of days |
| 4. Decision | Named approver (not the reviewer) | Accepts, corrects the record, or refers to accounts as a billing query | Before invoice approval |
| 5. Record | Reviewer | Outcome, reason and approver are logged against the item; the log is kept with the invoice | Same day as decision |
| 6. Appeal | Worker or contractor | Can ask for any decision to be re-examined by someone senior; the outcome is recorded | Before the next pay cycle |

Three rules make the workflow fair:

1. **A worker's pay is never reduced because of a reconciliation gap.** Differences are a billing matter between you and the contractor. If a worker was present and the contractor short-pays, section 55(3) means the liability can return to you.
2. **No automatic decisions.** A camera or device can list differences. Only a named person can decide what they mean, and every decision has a reason.
3. **Contract workers' data ends with the contract.** Face images or templates captured for gate matching should be deleted when the work order ends, while the attendance record itself is kept for the period your applicable rules require. See [attendance data: what to keep and what to delete](/insights/attendance-data-retention-what-to-delete).

## How do you enrol people who stay for two weeks?

Move enrolment to the gate and make it part of induction, which already happens. A supervised enrolment at the induction point, with a printed notice explaining what is collected and why, keeps the time per person low. Workers who object, or who cannot be enrolled, go on a supervised manual list that the reviewer checks each shift. Our guide to [seasonal workforce peaks](/insights/attendance-seasonal-peak-workforce) covers planning enrolment for large arrivals, and [onboarding a new joiner](/insights/onboarding-employee-into-attendance) covers the steps.

If you are evaluating face recognition on gate cameras for this, test it on your own gate first. Face-recognition attendance has no published PGAK evidence record yet, and identification depends on camera placement, light and coverings.

## Limitations

A gate record shows who passed a gate. It does not show where a person worked, whether they were productive, or whether the contractor paid them correctly. For wages, the evidence is the contractor's electronic payment record under section 55(2). For safety, you still need your own muster and evacuation process. And if fewer than fifty contract labour are engaged, Part I of Chapter XI may not apply at all, though the commercial case for reconciling invoices remains.
