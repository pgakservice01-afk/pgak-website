**Straight answer: compare reuse and replacement as two itemised five-year costs, not as old versus new. List every camera as kept, moved, replaced or added; price the recorder and cabling; add an allowance for the kept cameras that will fail during the five years; and keep the analytics lines separate because they come from the quotes. Age alone is almost never the deciding line — stream access, placement and physical condition are.**

Every supplier has a bias. A camera seller leans towards replacement. PGAK's business is adding analytics to cameras a site already owns, so we lean towards reuse, and you should allow for that exactly as you would for the other side. The defence against both biases is the same: an assessment camera by camera, with a reason for each.

## The four tests that decide each camera

**1. Does it produce a usable stream?** Analytics reads the stream from the camera or recorder. If an authorised stream cannot be opened for that channel, on that model and firmware, the camera cannot be analysed — however new it is.

**2. Can it see what matters?** A camera high over a doorway, angled steeply down, sees the tops of heads. The question is whether it can be repositioned, not whether it must be replaced. A ladder and an hour of labour cost less than a camera.

**3. Is the resolution adequate for the distance?** A camera that is fine for detecting a person in a 10-metre yard may be useless for reading a face at the far gate. Match each camera to the job you want from it.

**4. Is it physically sound?** Fogged housings, water inside, dead infrared, a sensor that has faded. These are visible on a live view and at night, and they are the clearest reason to replace.

Each camera ends up in one of four columns: **keep**, **move**, **replace** or **add** (a new position nothing currently covers).

## Itemised comparison: an illustrative example

The figures below are an **illustrative example** for a hypothetical 16-camera site. Every rupee value is a round, hypothetical input chosen to show the arithmetic — **not a market price and not a PGAK price**. Replace them with the figures from your own quotes.

**Hypothetical inputs**

| Input | Value used |
|---|---|
| Camera fitted on existing cable | ₹10,000 each |
| Repositioning labour | ₹2,000 per camera |
| Recorder | ₹40,000 |
| New cabling, for a full replacement | ₹3,000 per camera |
| Failure allowance, older cameras kept over five years | 1 in 4 (an assumption — use your own records) |
| Failure allowance, new cameras over five years | 1 in 16 (an assumption — check the warranty) |

The assessment (also hypothetical) finds: 12 cameras keep, 2 move, 2 have failed and must be replaced, 0 to add.

**Option A — retain and repair**

| Line | Arithmetic | Five-year cost |
|---|---|---|
| Keep 12 cameras | 12 × ₹0 | ₹0 |
| Move 2 cameras | 2 × ₹2,000 | ₹4,000 |
| Replace 2 failed cameras now | 2 × ₹10,000 | ₹20,000 |
| Failed-camera allowance for the 14 older cameras still in service | 14 ÷ 4 = 3.5, rounded up to 4 × ₹10,000 | ₹40,000 |
| Failed-camera allowance for the 2 new cameras | 2 ÷ 16 = 0.125, rounded down to 0 | ₹0 |
| Recorder replaced once during the period | 1 × ₹40,000 | ₹40,000 |
| **Total, before analytics** | | **₹1,04,000** |

**Option B — replace everything**

| Line | Arithmetic | Five-year cost |
|---|---|---|
| 16 new cameras | 16 × ₹10,000 | ₹1,60,000 |
| New recorder | 1 × ₹40,000 | ₹40,000 |
| New cabling | 16 × ₹3,000 | ₹48,000 |
| Failed-camera allowance for new cameras | 16 ÷ 16 = 1 × ₹10,000 | ₹10,000 |
| **Total, before analytics** | | **₹2,58,000** |

On these hypothetical inputs, retaining costs ₹1,54,000 less over five years (₹2,58,000 − ₹1,04,000), before the analytics lines.

**Stress-test the allowance.** The failure allowance is the assumption most likely to be wrong. If every one of the 14 older cameras failed during the period, Option A's allowance would be 14 × ₹10,000 = ₹1,40,000, and Option A would total ₹4,000 + ₹20,000 + ₹1,40,000 + ₹40,000 = ₹2,04,000 — still below Option B on these inputs. On your figures the answer may flip; that is the point of doing the arithmetic.

**Then add the analytics lines from the quotes.** Processing hardware, licence, installation and support usually appear in both options, so they do not change the gap unless they differ. They do differ if Option B uses cameras that run analytics on the camera itself; in that case put each supplier's processing and licence lines into the right column rather than assuming they cancel. PGAK's lines are quoted per site.

## What the arithmetic leaves out

- **Picture quality you need but do not have.** If the job is reading faces at the gate and no existing camera can, Option A needs an "add" line. Do not let a cheap Option A hide a camera it cannot do without.
- **Downtime during replacement.** A full replacement often means days of partial coverage.
- **Staff time** spent on repeated small repairs of an ageing estate.
- **An unsupported recorder.** If the recorder no longer receives firmware updates or cannot provide stable streams, budget its replacement in year one, not later.

## When replacement is the right answer

Replace when most cameras fail the stream or condition test, when the site needs recabling anyway, when the recorder cannot provide usable streams, or when the jobs you need (faces, plates) require positions the current estate does not have. In those cases a reuse project only delays the spend. Be equally sceptical of a supplier who will not say so.

## Before you accept any quote

Ask for one sentence per camera: kept, moved, replaced or added, and why. Ask whether repositioning was considered. Ask whether your recorder's stream access was actually tested, on its model and firmware.
