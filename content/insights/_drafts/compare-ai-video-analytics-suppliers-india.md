**Straight answer: compare AI video analytics suppliers in two stages. First, on what they publish — price and what it includes, pilot terms, which cameras they support, what proof sits behind their claims, and who is on the ground. Then, and more importantly, on a short pilot on your own cameras scored with the same weighted scorecard and the same pass/fail tests for every supplier. Website claims narrow the list; only the pilot decides.**

## What is the table really comparing?

**Three different purchases.** A ₹400-a-camera subscription that runs on a computer at your unit, a camera system priced in lakhs, and a quoted project that starts with an assessment are not one product at three prices. The [price guide](/insights/ai-cctv-price-in-india-what-it-should-cost) splits any quote into the same five lines so they can be compared.

**Pilots that answer different questions.** Seven days on 32 cameras tells you how a system copes across a site; thirty days on two tells you whether it can be trusted on the cameras that matter most. Choose the one that matches your question, and agree the scoring before it starts.

**A brand list versus your recorder.** "Works with 40 brands" is a fair opening, not a result. Two recorders with the same logo can differ by model and firmware. The [compatibility page](/platform/compatibility) lists what a proper check records.

**Percentages with no method.** A figure means something only with its site, period, baseline and whether it was measured or modelled. Ask every supplier, PGAK included. PGAK publishes no accuracy, uptime or response figure.

## The weighted vendor scorecard

Use one copy per supplier, filled in **after** the pilot, by the same person. Score each criterion 0 to 5. Multiply by the weight, add up, and divide by 5 to get a score out of 100. The weights are a suggestion — change them to suit your site, but fix them before the pilot starts.

| Criterion | Weight | What earns a 5 | Evidence source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fit on your cameras | 30 | High share of real events alerted, few false alerts, by day and night | Pilot log (tests T2–T3) |
| Cost clarity | 20 | All five quote lines itemised with tax per line; exit terms written | Written quote |
| Support and local response | 15 | Named contact, hours, response and resolution terms in writing | Proposal; T6 |
| Behaviour under failure | 10 | Clear, demonstrated behaviour when internet or a camera drops | T4–T5 |
| Evidence and transparency | 15 | Claims backed by a stated method; limits disclosed | Website; proposal |
| Data handling and exit | 10 | Where video and events are stored, who can access them, what you keep at exit | Proposal; T7 |
| **Total** | **100** | | |

## Named pass/fail pilot tests

Some tests are **must-pass**: a fail removes the supplier regardless of score. Write the pass condition next to each before the pilot begins.

| Test | What you do | Pass condition (write yours in) | Must-pass? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Oldest-camera stream** | Supplier connects to your two oldest cameras through an authorised process | Stable analysis on both for the whole pilot, or a written reason per camera | Yes |
| **T2 Night test** | Staged and real events logged after dark on the cameras that matter | Agreed share of eligible night events alerted | Yes |
| **T3 Ground-truth log** | A person logs every eligible event independently of the system; compare | False and missed alerts counted, not estimated | Yes |
| **T4 Internet outage** | Disconnect the site's internet for an agreed period | Behaviour matches what the supplier said in writing beforehand | Yes |
| **T5 Camera unplugged** | Unplug one camera without warning | You are told, by the route and within the time promised in writing — or the supplier said in advance it does not offer this | No |
| **T6 Rule change** | Ask for a zone or schedule change mid-pilot | Change made and confirmed within the agreed time | No |
| **T7 Export** | Ask for an export of all pilot events | Received in a usable format with timestamps | No |

The method for T3 — counting true, false and missed alerts and measuring delay — is set out in the [pilot worksheet](/resources/evaluation-method).

## Worked scorecard: an illustrative example

Two hypothetical suppliers, X and Y, after the same pilot. These are invented scores to show the arithmetic, not an assessment of any real supplier.

| Criterion | Weight | X score | X weighted | Y score | Y weighted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fit on your cameras | 30 | 4 | 120 | 3 | 90 |
| Cost clarity | 20 | 4 | 80 | 3 | 60 |
| Support and local response | 15 | 2 | 30 | 4 | 60 |
| Behaviour under failure | 10 | 5 | 50 | 3 | 30 |
| Evidence and transparency | 15 | 3 | 45 | 4 | 60 |
| Data handling and exit | 10 | 3 | 30 | 4 | 40 |
| **Sum ÷ 5** | | | **355 ÷ 5 = 71** | | **340 ÷ 5 = 68** |

X scores higher. But suppose X failed **T1** — one of the two oldest cameras could not hold a stable stream and X gave no written reason. T1 is must-pass, so X is out, and Y is the choice despite the lower total. That is the point of separating the gates from the score: a high average should not hide a failure you cannot live with.

## Limitations

A two-week pilot on a few cameras does not prove performance across a year of seasons, or at another site. Staged events are easier than real ones. Score with what you observed, and keep the pilot log — it is the baseline for judging the full rollout. And if none of the suppliers passes the must-pass tests, buying nothing yet is a legitimate outcome.
