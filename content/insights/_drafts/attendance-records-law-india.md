**Straight answer: there is no one retention period for attendance records in India, and anyone who quotes a single number for every business is guessing. Since 21 November 2025 the labour codes require employers to keep attendance registers; the period comes from the rules of whichever government is the "appropriate Government" for your establishment. Central rules notified on 8 May 2026 say five years after the last entry — but many private establishments fall under state rules instead. Biometric data used to *produce* the register follows a different logic under the DPDP Act, from May 2027. Work out which rules apply to you, record the source, and set your periods from that.**

This is the question behind "how long do we keep attendance data?" — and it has changed since many articles on it were written, because the four labour codes have replaced the old Acts.

## What changed on 21 November 2025?

The Ministry of Labour and Employment brought the four labour codes into force on 21 November 2025:

- **Code on Wages, 2019** — principal provisions in force by S.O. 5322(E), 21 November 2025, including sections 1 to 41 and 43 to 66.
- **Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (OSH Code)** — in force by S.O. 5321(E), 21 November 2025.
- The Industrial Relations Code, 2020 and the Code on Social Security, 2020 came in the same week; their own record-keeping rules are not covered here.

Section 143(1) of the OSH Code repeals, among others, the Factories Act, 1948 and the Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970. Section 143(3) says rules and notifications made under the repealed Acts remain in force "to the extent they are not contrary to the provisions of this Code till they are repealed by the Central Government". The Ministry's FAQs on the Labour Codes (published 30 December 2025) say the same in plain terms: old rules continue until new rules are notified, to the extent they are in line with the Codes. <!-- UNRESOLVED: confirm the official URL of the MoLE FAQs dated 30 Dec 2025. -->

## Which laws require an attendance register?

Two provisions in the codes matter most:

- **Code on Wages, 2019, section 50(1):** every employer must maintain a register of persons employed, muster roll, wages and other prescribed details. Rule 51 of the **Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026** (G.S.R. 343(E), 8 May 2026) prescribes three registers — an Employee Register (Form I), a Register of Wages, Overtime, Advances, Fines and Deductions (Form IV), and an **Attendance Register-cum-Muster Roll (Form IX)** with daily in and out times — kept "electronically or in physical form". Rule 51(4): "preserved for a period of five years after the date of last entry made therein". <!-- UNRESOLVED: s.50 text read via rule 51 reference and secondary summaries; India Code unavailable on 8 Oct 2026. -->
- **OSH Code, 2020, section 33(a):** the employer must maintain a register, "electronically or otherwise", with prescribed particulars including attendance, hours of work, leave and overtime. The particulars and form are prescribed by the appropriate Government.

## Whose rules apply to you?

The Central Rules apply where the Central Government is the appropriate Government under the Code. For many private factories and shops, the appropriate Government is expected to be the State Government, whose own rules set the forms and periods. Confirm this for your establishment. <!-- UNRESOLVED: definition of 'appropriate Government' in s.2 of the Code on Wages not read directly. -->

For Punjab: on 8 October 2026 the Punjab Labour Department website (pblabour.gov.in) still listed the older rules — including the Punjab Factories Rules, 1952, the Payment of Wages and Minimum Wages rules, and the shops act rules — under "Act & Rules". We did not find final Punjab rules under the Code on Wages or the OSH Code there. Draft Punjab rules were reported in late December 2025; we could not open an official copy. <!-- UNRESOLVED: check Punjab Gazette for final Code on Wages (Punjab) and OSH (Punjab) rules. -->

## Why does the DPDP Act pull the other way?

Labour law says *keep the register*. From May 2027 data protection law says *do not keep personal data longer than its purpose needs*. They stop conflicting once you separate the two things:

- **The register** — who was present, when, for how many hours — is the record a law requires. Section 8(7) of the DPDP Act expressly allows retention "necessary for compliance with any law for the time being in force".
- **The biometric template** that matched the person at the gate is a means to produce the register. Once the person has left and final dues are settled, there is usually no purpose left for it. Rule 8(3) of the DPDP Rules, 2025 separately requires personal data and logs of processing to be kept for at least one year for specified government purposes, so check with your adviser how that interacts with deleting templates on exit. <!-- UNRESOLVED: counsel to confirm how DPDP Rules r.8(3) applies to biometric templates after exit. -->

Systems that store raw biometric data indefinitely as part of the "attendance archive" create risk with no compliance benefit. [How to delete it without breaking the audit trail](/insights/attendance-data-retention-what-to-delete).

## Contract labour

The Contract Labour (Regulation and Abolition) Act, 1970 is one of the Acts repealed by section 143 of the OSH Code. Who must keep which records for contract workers now depends on the OSH Code and the rules that apply to you. We have not reviewed those provisions for this article, so we make no claim about them — ask your adviser specifically whether contractors' workers must appear in your registers. [Practical issues with contract labour attendance](/insights/attendance-system-for-contract-labour).

## Limitations

This article covers attendance and wage registers under two codes and the DPDP Act. It does not cover the Code on Social Security or the Industrial Relations Code, sector rules, or tax and accounting retention, any of which may require you to keep related records longer. If you already keep paper registers that satisfy your inspector, a new system is not required by any law we found — only that whatever you use produces the prescribed register.
