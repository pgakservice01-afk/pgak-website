**Straight answer: every face-recognition system will sometimes match the wrong person and sometimes miss the right one. That is not a defect in one product; it is how the technology works, and the rate depends on your cameras, light and enrolment. So before you buy, write down what a human checks, who can overrule the system, and how an employee appeals — and make it a rule that a match alone never cuts pay, records misconduct or locks someone out. Ask every supplier to show how their system supports that process.**

Face recognition gets sold on speed: no queue, no touching, attendance marked as people walk in. That is real. What gets less attention is the morning a supervisor sees an employee marked absent who was standing at the gate, or marked present when they were on leave. What happens next is a policy question, and it should be answered before the first payroll run.

## Why do false matches and misses happen?

Because a camera at a gate is not a passport counter. Faces turn, caps and helmets cover foreheads, a low winter sun washes out half a face, the enrolment photo was taken under office lights. Lookalike relatives and twins exist. Settings that cut false matches usually raise misses, and the reverse. [What affects face recognition accuracy](/insights/face-recognition-accuracy-what-affects-it) and [masks, helmets and turbans](/insights/masks-helmets-turbans-face-recognition) cover the causes; [whether a photo can fool it](/insights/can-a-photo-fool-face-attendance) covers spoofing.

The consequence is the same whatever the cause: a person has to be able to look at the evidence and decide.

## What do official sources say?

There is no Indian rule we found written specifically for face recognition at work. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Gazette, 11 August 2023) contains provisions that point the same way, from May 2027 when they commence under notification G.S.R. 843(E) of 13 November 2025:

- **Section 8(3):** where personal data "is likely to be used to make a decision that affects the Data Principal", the Data Fiduciary must "ensure its completeness, accuracy and consistency".
- **Section 12:** a right to correction, completion and updating of personal data, for data processed on consent (including under section 7(a)).
- **Section 13:** a right to "readily available means of grievance redressal". Rule 14(3) of the DPDP Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 November 2025) asks for grievances to be answered within a published period "not exceeding ninety days".

Which of these apply to your use depends on your legal basis, which is for your adviser. <!-- UNRESOLVED: counsel to confirm application of ss.8(3), 12, 13 to employer face recognition. --> A review and appeal process is sensible whatever the answer.

## The human-review and appeal policy (the asset)

A starting draft. Fit the names and times to your site, and have your adviser review it before rollout.

**1. What the system may do on its own**
- Record a *proposed* attendance mark, with the frame it came from.
- Flag an unrecognised person in a restricted area for a guard to look at.
- Nothing else. It may not deduct pay, record misconduct, or keep a gate locked against an enrolled employee without a person deciding.

**2. Daily review (before marks reach payroll)**
- A named HR or admin person reviews exceptions each day: unmatched entries, people marked absent who were rostered, two marks for one person at different gates within minutes, marks for people on leave.
- For each exception they look at the stored frame and decide: confirm, correct, or ask the supervisor.
- Every correction records who made it, when and why.

**3. Before any adverse action**
- No deduction, warning or disciplinary note based on face-recognition records unless a second person has checked the frames and the employee has been told and given a chance to explain.
- If frames are unclear, the record is treated as unresolved, not as absence.

**4. Gate entry**
- If the system does not recognise an enrolled employee, the guard checks their ID card or calls a supervisor. A "no match" is never a reason to keep someone outside on its own.
- An alternative — card, PIN, guard entry — is available to anyone the system repeatedly fails or who does not wish to be enrolled. [When an employee refuses biometric attendance](/insights/employee-refuses-biometric-consent).

**5. Appeal**
- Named contact (role, phone, email) shown on the attendance notice.
- The employee can raise a challenge in writing — a form, a message, a note to HR.
- Reviewed by someone who did not make the original decision.
- Answer within a fixed period you publish (for example, before the next payroll run).
- If the employee is right: correct the record, reverse any pay effect, and tell them in writing.

**6. Monthly error log**
- Count corrections by gate, shift and person. A gate with many corrections needs camera or lighting work; a person corrected repeatedly may need re-enrolment or the alternative method.
- Review false matches separately: two different people confused is more serious than a missed match.

**7. Annual check**
- Re-test at each gate in real conditions (morning, evening, monsoon light) and re-enrol people whose appearance has changed.

## What to ask a supplier

1. Does every mark store the frame that produced it, and for how long?
2. Can marks be held as "proposed" until reviewed, rather than going straight to payroll?
3. Does the system log who corrected what, and when?
4. Can the match setting be changed per gate, and who can change it?
5. What happens at the gate when a known employee is not recognised?
6. Can you show us a week of real exceptions from a pilot at our gates?

A supplier who can only quote an accuracy percentage from a datasheet has not answered any of these.

## When not to use face recognition

If your workforce is small, your gate is dark or crowded, or the people you need to recognise are visitors rather than enrolled staff, a card reader, a register or guard checks may serve you better. If the main goal is knowing *that* someone is in a restricted area rather than *who*, person detection without identification avoids most of these problems.

## Limitations

A review process adds HR time, especially in the first weeks. It does not make the system more accurate; it stops inaccuracies becoming unfair decisions. PGAK has no published accuracy figure for face recognition; whether it works at your gates is assessed per site, with camera suitability, stream and processing hardware confirmed before any quote.
