**Straight answer: we found no Indian law that prescribes the wording, size or placement of a CCTV sign. A clear notice is still the sensible default — it tells people their image is being recorded, gives them someone to ask, and from May 2027 may be part of meeting the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 if your cameras rely on consent. The rule that matters most is simple: every line on the sign must match what your system actually does. A sign that promises 30 days when your recorder keeps 9 is worse than no promise at all.**

You have put cameras up, and someone — an employee, a visitor, an auditor — asks whether you need a sign. Here is what we could and could not verify.

## What does the law say about CCTV signs?

- **No dedicated CCTV signage law found.** We did not find a central Act or rule that specifies a CCTV notice. We have not searched every state police order, municipal rule or sector regulation, so a requirement may exist for your state or industry. <!-- UNRESOLVED: state and sector signage rules not checked. -->
- **DPDP Act notice duty.** Section 5 of the DPDP Act (Gazette, 11 August 2023) requires "every request made to a Data Principal under section 6 for consent" to be accompanied or preceded by a notice describing the personal data and purpose, how to exercise rights, and how to complain to the Data Protection Board. Section 5(3) requires it to be available in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution. Rule 3 of the DPDP Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E), 13 November 2025) adds that the notice must be understandable on its own and give an itemised description of the data and the purposes.
- **Timing.** Sections 5 and 6 and rule 3 all commence in May 2027 under notifications G.S.R. 843(E) and 846(E) of 13 November 2025.
- **Consent or legitimate use?** The section 5 duty is tied to consent. Processing for a legitimate use under section 7 — for example section 7(i), employment purposes — is not described in the Act as needing a section 5 notice. Whether your visitor cameras or staff cameras rely on consent, a legitimate use, or something else is a question for counsel. <!-- UNRESOLVED: counsel to advise basis for visitor CCTV and whether s.5 applies. -->

So the honest summary is: a notice is good practice now, may become part of a legal duty from May 2027 depending on your basis, and is cheap. It is not a single nationwide legal formula.

## What should the notice explain?

Whatever the legal basis, a reader has five questions: who is recording, why, what happens to the footage, is anything automated, and whom do I ask? A camera icon with no text answers none of them.

## The notice template, linked to your real settings (the asset)

Fill the right-hand column *from your system*, not from memory. If you cannot check a line, leave it off the sign until you can.

| Line on the sign | Where to check it before printing |
|---|---|
| **"CCTV in operation. Operated by [legal name of business]."** | Your registered business name — not the installer's. |
| **"For the safety of people and property and to investigate incidents."** | Write only purposes you actually use footage for. If you also use cameras for attendance, say so on the staff notice, not hidden here. |
| **"Areas covered: [entrance, yard, loading bay, cash counter]."** | Walk the site with the recorder's camera list. Remove any camera that sees into a neighbour's property or any private space. |
| **"Recordings are normally kept for about [N] days and then overwritten."** | The recorder's storage page shows actual retention. Use the lower of what it shows and what it held last month. [How to check real retention](/insights/cctv-storage-how-many-days). |
| **"Footage is viewed by [roles] and shared only with [police on request / insurer / as required by law]."** | Your access list on the recorder. If ten people share one login, fix that first. |
| **"Some cameras use automated detection [people/vehicles in restricted areas]."** | Only if true. Say whether any camera identifies people by face. Do not hide face recognition behind "AI". |
| **"Questions or requests: [name or role], [phone], [email]."** | A person who actually answers, not the installer's helpline. |

A filled example, for a hypothetical Ludhiana warehouse (illustrative example):

> **CCTV in operation**
> Operated by ABC Warehousing Pvt. Ltd. for the safety of people and property and to investigate incidents.
> Cameras cover this gate, the yard and the loading bays.
> Recordings are normally kept for about 15 days and then overwritten. They are viewed by our security supervisor and manager, and shared only with police or our insurer when needed.
> Cameras detect people in the yard after hours. No camera identifies people by face.
> Questions: Security Manager, [phone], [email].

Print it in English and in the languages your visitors and staff read — for most Punjab sites, Punjabi and Hindi as well.

**Staff notice board.** Employees need fuller information than visitors: which cameras cover work areas, whether footage is used in disciplinary matters, whether any camera is used for attendance or face recognition, and how long each kind of record is kept. If face attendance is involved, that is a separate notice — [what an attendance notice should contain](/insights/employee-refuses-biometric-consent).

## Where should the signs go?

- At every public entrance a camera actually covers, before the monitored area.
- At separately monitored areas such as parking, loading bays or a cash counter, with the purpose for that area.
- On the staff notice board, with the fuller employee wording.
- Never as a substitute for keeping cameras out of washrooms and changing areas. Section 77 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 makes capturing images of a woman in a private act, including using a lavatory, an offence.

## Does a visible sign help security?

It may deter some opportunistic wrongdoing, but we have no measurement for Indian sites and do not claim one. A sign at a covered entrance says nothing about what is uncovered elsewhere. It will not stop someone who has already planned around your cameras — [where they look for gaps](/insights/cctv-blind-spots-where-thieves-look).

## Limitations

A sign is a description of your practices, not a fix for them. If retention is unknown, access is a shared password, or cameras see places they should not, the sign will be inaccurate or will advertise a problem. Fix the practice first; the sign then takes ten minutes. We supply and configure camera analytics; we do not draft legal notices, so treat the template as a starting draft for your adviser.
