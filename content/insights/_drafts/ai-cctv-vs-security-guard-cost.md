**Straight answer: AI CCTV and security guards do different jobs, so the comparison is about tasks, not headcount. Software can watch many camera views at once and raise an alert; a guard intervenes, challenges, helps people and makes judgement calls. Keep every post your site is required to staff and every intervention task with a person. Then the cost question becomes narrow and honest: when you need more coverage, is analytics on your existing cameras plus your current guards responding a better use of money than adding another 24-hour post?**

This is one of the most common questions we hear, usually framed as "should I hire another guard or install your system?" Here is the version that keeps your site safe while answering it.

## What a guard does that software cannot

- **Physical intervention.** Someone has to walk to the gate, stop a vehicle, or stand between a stranger and a store room.
- **Judgement in ambiguity.** A transporter arriving an hour early with a plausible story is a human problem.
- **Helping people.** First response in an injury, a fire or an evacuation. No camera does this.
- **Presence.** A visible, alert guard at the gate changes behaviour before anything needs detecting.
- **The many small tasks** around a gate: visitor entries, keys, material gate passes, fire panel checks, calling the supervisor.

## What software does that a guard cannot

- **Look at every camera view continuously.** A person can attend to one view at a time, and watching a wall of screens for a full night is hard for anyone.
- **Apply the same rule at 3 a.m. as at 9 p.m.** Once a rule is set and tested, it does not drift with fatigue.
- **Keep a record.** Each alert has a time, a camera and a snapshot that can be checked later.

Software does none of these perfectly. Rules miss events and raise false ones, which is why they must be tested on your site — see [reducing false alarms without hiding real incidents](/insights/ai-cctv-false-alarms-how-to-reduce). Perimeter intrusion alerting in particular has no published PGAK evidence record yet, so treat it as a capability to evaluate at your site, not an assumption.

## Staffing you must keep, whatever you buy

Before any comparison, list the posts that are not optional for your site. Check, rather than assume:

- posts written into a **client or principal-employer contract** (common for units supplying large buyers);
- conditions in your **insurance policy** for stock, fire or burglary cover;
- your **fire-safety and emergency plan**, including who raises the alarm and leads evacuation on each shift;
- anything your **security agency's agreement** or a licence condition specifies;
- a plain **safety assessment**: no one should be alone at night on a large site without a way to call for help.

Those posts stay. The worksheet below only reallocates tasks *within* the staffing you keep, or replaces a post you were *planning to add*.

## Task allocation worksheet

Fill one row per task, per shift. The last two columns are the point: who must still do it, and whether that is retained.

| Task | Who does it today | Hours per night shift | Can camera analytics assist? | Who must still do it | Retained with a person? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gate: visitor and vehicle entries | Gate guard | 2 | Partly — vehicle logging, if evaluated | Gate guard | Yes |
| Watching the monitor wall | Gate guard | 5 | Yes — alerts on armed zones replace continuous watching | Guard checks each alert | Yes — verification |
| Rear wall and yard observation | Nobody (no line of sight) | 0 | Yes — zone rules, if your cameras can carry them | Guard responds to alerts | Yes — response |
| Patrol rounds | Gate guard | 2 | No | Gate guard | Yes |
| Responding to an alarm or alert | Gate guard + supervisor by phone | as needed | No | Guard and supervisor | Yes |
| Fire panel and emergency first response | Gate guard | 0.5 | No | Gate guard | Yes |
| Checking that cameras are working | Nobody | 0 | Possibly — evaluate camera-health alerts on site | Supervisor reviews daily | Yes — review |
| Register and handover notes | Gate guard | 0.5 | No | Gate guard | Yes |

*Illustrative example.* In this hypothetical night shift, the guard spends about 5 of his hours looking at the monitor wall, and nobody can see the rear wall at all. If analytics takes the first look at the cameras, those 5 hours do not disappear — they move to **patrols, response and verification**, which are the tasks only he can do. Same post, same person, more of the boundary covered.

## The cost comparison, done properly

Only run this when you were going to **add** coverage — say a second night post for the rear of the site.

**Step 1: what one 24-hour post really needs (illustrative example).**

- Three 8-hour shifts per day: 3 people on duty.
- Each takes one weekly off: cover needed for 3 × 1 = 3 shift-days a week, which is half of one more person (3 ÷ 6 working days).
- Leave and absence: add a further margin your agency or roster shows.
- 3 + 0.5 + margin ≈ **4 people** per round-the-clock post.

**Step 2: its monthly cost (illustrative example).** Use your agency's invoice. With a round placeholder of ₹20,000 per guard per month — not a market rate — four people cost 4 × ₹20,000 = **₹80,000 a month**, or ₹9,60,000 a year. Night-only posts change the arithmetic; work out your own.

**Step 3: what the analytics option really includes.** Itemise it; never compare against a single headline number.

| Component | Who supplies it | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| On-site processing unit | Supplier | Quoted per site |
| Software licence and support | Supplier | Quoted per site |
| Camera repositioning or additions where a view cannot carry a rule | Installer | From the site survey |
| Lighting improvements for night detection | Electrician | From the site survey |
| Network work to the processing unit | Installer | From the site survey |
| Alert review time | Your existing guard and supervisor | Their time — use the calculators below |
| Response | Your existing guard | Already paid — but it must remain available |

PGAK publishes no per-camera or package price; the processing hardware, compatibility of your cameras, stream access and network are confirmed at the assessment, and anything extra is itemised in the quote.

**Step 4: compare like with like.** "One more post" against "analytics plus the response capacity you already have", over the same period, and including the review time the alerts will take. If the existing guard cannot leave the gate to respond, analytics alone does not deliver the coverage the extra post would have, and the comparison should say so.

## When a guard, or nothing new, is the better answer

- **If the gap is response, not detection** — nobody can attend at night — add a person or a response arrangement first.
- **If your cameras cannot see the area** (no line of sight, no lighting), analytics has nothing to work with; fix placement or lighting, or post a person. The [blind-spot site-plan exercise](/insights/cctv-blind-spots-where-thieves-look) shows how to check.
- **If the existing guard is overloaded at the gate**, analytics will add alerts he cannot act on.
- **If nothing has happened and the risk is low**, better locks, lighting and a working recorder may be enough.
