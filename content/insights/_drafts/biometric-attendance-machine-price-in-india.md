**Straight answer: the price of a biometric attendance machine is the smallest of five costs you will pay. The others are the software licence, enrolment time, support and replacement, and getting attendance into payroll. Prices and bundles change often, so compare suppliers on a single procurement sheet covering all five over the years you expect to use the system, not on the device price in a listing.**

Search for "biometric attendance machine price" and you will find hundreds of listings. They are hard to compare because each one bundles something different. We checked several dealer listings on 8 October 2026: one bundled a year of cloud software for a capped number of employees, with more employees "on enquiry"; another showed a sale price higher than the MRP printed beside it, and GST treatment was not clear. That is why this article gives you a sheet to fill in rather than a price band.

## What are you actually buying?

An attendance system has five parts, and a device price covers only the first.

1. **The device** at each entrance: fingerprint, card, face terminal, or a combination.
2. **The licence**: the software that turns punches into attendance, shifts, overtime and reports. It may be bundled for a year, charged per employee, per device, or as a flat subscription.
3. **Enrolment**: the time to register every employee, and every new joiner after that.
4. **Support**: AMC, repairs, replacement sensors or terminals, and who comes when it fails.
5. **Integration**: getting a clean, rule-applied file into payroll every month. See [connecting attendance to payroll](/insights/biometric-attendance-payroll-integration).

Leave out any one of these and two quotes that look similar can be very different over five years.

## The procurement sheet

Give this sheet to every supplier and ask them to fill in every row. A blank is an answer too.

| # | Line item | What to ask | Supplier A | Supplier B | One-time or yearly? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Devices | How many entrances need a device, including side gates and loading bays? Price per device, GST included or not? | | | One-time |
| 2 | Mounting, wiring, network, power | Is installation included? Who runs cable and provides a network point and power backup? | | | One-time |
| 3 | Access control extras | Lock, exit button, bracket, battery: included or extra? | | | One-time |
| 4 | Software licence | Per employee, per device or flat? What is included in year one, and what renews? Is there a cap on employees? | | | Yearly |
| 5 | Cloud or local hosting | Where are records stored? Is there a hosting fee after year one? | | | Yearly |
| 6 | Enrolment | Who enrols staff, and how long per person? How are new joiners and contract workers enrolled? | | | One-time and ongoing |
| 7 | Payroll export | Does it export hours already split into regular and overtime, or only raw punches? Any integration charge? | | | One-time |
| 8 | Rules set-up | Who configures shifts, grace, overtime and holidays? Is reconfiguration charged? | | | One-time and on change |
| 9 | Support | AMC price, what it covers, response time stated in writing, spare device policy | | | Yearly |
| 10 | Replacement | Expected sensor or device life in your conditions; replacement price | | | Every few years |
| 11 | Exit and data | Can you export all history if you leave? How are biometric templates deleted on exit? | | | One-time |

### Turning the sheet into a five-year number

Use your own figures from the sheet:

- **One-time** = (devices × entrances) + installation + extras + integration + set-up
- **Yearly** = licence + hosting + AMC
- **Five-year total** = one-time + (yearly × 5) + expected replacements within five years

Then add the line nobody quotes: **staff time**. Count the hours someone spends each month fixing missed punches and preparing payroll. The [attendance administration time calculator](/calculators/attendance-admin-time) turns that into hours and, if you enter an hourly cost, a capacity value. Keep it separate from cash; hours become money only if something actually changes, such as overtime that stops.

## Where do the hidden costs usually hide?

**Entrance count.** A quote for one device covers one door. Many sites have a main gate, a side gate and a loading bay, and people use whichever is nearest.

**The licence after year one.** "Includes one year of software" means year two is a new bill. Ask for the renewal price in writing.

**Employee caps.** A bundle "for up to 50 employees" may not cover a site with 80 permanent staff and seasonal contract labour.

**Enrolment of contract workers.** If enrolment needs an office visit, short-tenure workers often end up on a paper list instead. See [attendance for contract labour](/insights/attendance-system-for-contract-labour).

**The manual-entry fallback.** If some fingers will not read, a PIN, card or supervisor entry fills the gap. That costs reviewer time every month and weakens the record. See [why fingerprint attendance fails at industrial gates](/insights/fingerprint-attendance-system-why-it-fails).

## What about using CCTV cameras instead?

If your entrances already have cameras, face recognition on those cameras can be evaluated as an alternative. It changes the cost lines rather than removing them:

| Line | What changes with camera-based attendance |
|---|---|
| Devices | No attendance terminal, but a camera that sees faces at a usable size at each entrance. Some existing cameras qualify; many overview cameras do not and need a dedicated camera at face height. |
| Processing | Detection runs on a processing unit on site. Whether one is needed, and its specification, is confirmed at assessment. |
| Licence | Software licence, quoted per site. PGAK publishes no per-camera or package price. |
| Enrolment | Still needed for every employee and new joiner. |
| Support and integration | Still needed. Ask the same questions as rows 7–11. |

Face-recognition attendance has no published PGAK evidence record yet. Treat it as something to test on your own gate, alongside your current system, before you retire anything. The comparison article [face attendance or a fingerprint machine](/insights/face-recognition-attendance-vs-biometric-machine) includes a two-week test.

## When is a basic machine the right answer?

If you have one entrance, a small team with clean hands, staggered arrivals and simple shifts, a mid-range fingerprint machine with a decent payroll export may serve you for years. Fill in the sheet anyway; the licence and support rows still matter.
