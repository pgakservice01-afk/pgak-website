**Straight answer: when an employee refuses fingerprint or face attendance, treat it as a process question, not a discipline question. Record the refusal, explain in writing what the data is used for, and offer an alternative method that produces the same attendance record. Whether Indian law requires consent for your system — or permits it under the employment "legitimate use" in the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 — is for your counsel to decide, and most of that Act's duties start only in May 2027. Have the alternative ready either way.**

This comes up more often than HR teams expect. Someone objects to fingerprint scanning for religious or personal reasons. A new joiner asks what happens to their face data when they leave. A union raises it during rollout. The instinct is to issue a warning. That usually turns a data question into an industrial-relations dispute.

## What does the law actually say?

Less than many articles claim, and more carefully worded.

- **Two grounds, not one.** Section 4 of the DPDP Act, 2023 (Gazette, 11 August 2023) allows processing for a lawful purpose either with consent or "for certain legitimate uses". Section 7(i) lists "the purposes of employment or those related to safeguarding the employer from loss or liability". The Act does not create a special category for biometric data, and it does not say in terms that employers must or need not take consent for attendance.
- **If consent is the basis**, section 6(1) requires it to be "free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous", and section 6(4) lets the person withdraw it as easily as it was given. Consent that is a condition of keeping one's job is hard to call free — which is one reason employers look at section 7(i) instead. Which route fits your site is a legal judgment. <!-- UNRESOLVED: counsel to confirm basis for biometric attendance under s.4/s.7(i). -->
- **Timing.** Under MeitY notification G.S.R. 843(E) of 13 November 2025, sections 4 to 9 commence eighteen months after publication — May 2027.
- **Until then**, the IT Act, 2000 regime (section 43A and the 2011 rules on sensitive personal data, which the DPDP Act will remove when its section 44(2) commences in the same May 2027 batch) may still apply to companies collecting biometrics. <!-- UNRESOLVED: official text of the 2011 SPDI Rules not opened on 8 Oct 2026; counsel to confirm current consent requirements for biometric attendance. -->

We found no provision that says, in so many words, that an employer must offer a non-biometric alternative. We recommend one anyway, for the practical reasons below — and because, if consent turns out to be your basis, an alternative is what makes the consent meaningful.

## Why is forcing it the wrong fight?

**Coerced enrolment produces bad data.** Someone who resents the scan will find ways to make it fail — a badly angled face, a smudged finger — and you now have a data-quality problem on top of the objection.

**The goal was never "everyone enrolled".** It was "we know who is on site and when". An alternative that answers that honestly gets you the outcome without the standoff.

## The alternative attendance workflow (the asset)

Use this as a starting draft. Have your adviser review it against your standing orders before adopting it.

| Step | When | Who | What happens | Record kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Refusal noted | Day 0 | Supervisor / HR | Employee says they will not enrol. No argument at the gate. | Date, name, method refused. No reason demanded. |
| 2. Alternative issued | Same day | HR / admin | Card, fob or personal PIN issued, or supervisor-witnessed entry enabled in the same system. | Credential ID linked to employee code. |
| 3. Written explanation | Within a week | HR | Plain-language notice (template below) in a language the employee reads. Offer to answer questions. | Copy of notice, date given. |
| 4. Conversation offered | Within two weeks | HR + employee (union rep if they wish) | Explain what is stored, who sees it, when it is deleted. Ask whether a specific worry can be resolved. | Short note of outcome. |
| 5. Choice respected | Ongoing | HR | If the employee still declines, the alternative continues. Attendance is judged on the alternative's record. | Same reports as biometric users. |
| 6. Escalation — only if attendance is in doubt | When records show a real problem | Manager + HR | Address the *attendance* issue (absence, lateness, buddy-punching on the card) through the normal process. The refusal itself is not the charge. | Normal attendance case file. |
| 7. Review | Every quarter | HR | How many refusals? If rising, treat it as a trust problem: what are people worried about? | Count only, no names, in the review note. |

**The test for a genuine alternative:** does it produce a comparable, auditable record, and is it available to anyone who asks? An alternative that is slower, visibly singles people out, or needs a manager's permission every day defeats the purpose.

**The honest weakness:** a card or PIN can be shared. If buddy-punching on the alternative becomes a real problem, deal with it as an attendance problem — supervisor checks, gate camera review — rather than by forcing biometrics. ## Notice template

Adapt it to your actual system. Every line must be true of what your system really does; if you cannot fill a line in, that is a gap to fix before rollout.

> **Attendance at [company name]: what we collect and why**
>
> We record attendance at [gate names] using [fingerprint / face recognition / card]. For face or fingerprint attendance we store [a face template / a fingerprint template], not [a photo gallery], linked to your employee code.
>
> **Why:** to record start and end times for wages and statutory registers.
>
> **Who can see it:** [HR role] and [payroll role]. Security staff [can / cannot] see attendance records.
>
> **How long:** the template is deleted within [number] days of your last working day. Attendance records (dates and times) are kept for [period] because [the register rule that applies to us].
>
> **If you prefer not to enrol:** ask [name/role] for a [card / PIN]. Your attendance will be recorded in the same way and reported the same way.
>
> **Questions or complaints:** [name, role, phone/email].

Translate it into the languages your workforce reads. From May 2027, where consent is the basis, section 5(3) of the DPDP Act requires the notice to be available in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution, and rule 3 of the DPDP Rules, 2025 sets out what a consent notice must contain.

## Where does face recognition fit?

We supply face-recognition attendance, so weigh this with that interest in mind. Face enrolment is the same kind of step as fingerprint enrolment and needs the same notice, basis and alternative. It can cause more anxiety than a scanner because people can see the camera all day, not only at the gate. A short, accurate notice at enrolment — what is captured, what it is used for, when it is deleted — answers most objections before they start. Face-recognition attendance is something PGAK can evaluate at your site; camera suitability and processing hardware are confirmed at the assessment.

## Limitations

No attendance system, biometric or not, removes the need for a human process when someone cannot or will not use the main method — injured hands, worn fingerprints, a covered face for religious reasons. Budget for the alternative from day one. And if more than a handful of people refuse, a better notice will not fix it; find out what they are worried about.
