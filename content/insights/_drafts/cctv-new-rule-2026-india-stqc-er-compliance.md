**Straight answer: the CCTV security rule governs cameras under BIS registration — what is made, imported and sold — not cameras already on your wall. We found nothing in the official notifications requiring installed, working cameras to be removed. For new purchases, the check is model by model: the exact model on your quotation should appear on BIS's public list of CCTV models complying with the security Essential Requirements, under a registration that is still valid. A badge, a brochure line or "STQC certified" in an email is not that check.**

Business owners have been receiving calls and forwards saying the government has banned their cameras and everything must be replaced. For a factory with thirty or forty cameras, that is a large number with a deadline the owner has had no chance to check. Here is what the official documents say, dated, and what is only reported.

So "the 2026 rule" is, at most, the end of a grace period for old stock — and the document that ends it is one we could not read in official form. The security requirements themselves date from 2024–25.

## Who does the rule apply to?

The ER were added to the Compulsory Registration Order, which works through BIS registration of products. The BIS circular of 1 April 2025 shows the practical effect: after 9 April 2025, non-analog models without ER compliance were removed from manufacturers' licence scope. That is a restriction on making and selling registered products. We found no provision that reaches back to cameras already bought and installed.

Where it affects you is the next purchase: new cameras, an expansion, replacing a failed unit.

## What do the Essential Requirements cover?

The annexure to S.O. 1652(E) lists five areas: physical security (tamper-resistant enclosures), access control (authentication, role-based access control, regular review of permissions), network security (encrypted transmission), software security (regular updates, disabling unused features, strong password policies) and penetration testing. The detailed test table includes checks on debug interfaces, secure boot, encrypted communication, the firmware update process and review of code — including third-party libraries — "for hardcoded credentials (backdoors)".

The concern is real: a camera is a computer on your network with a lens attached. A badly built one adds a way in.

## Where does STQC fit?

STQC is a MeitY directorate. Its published "High-Level process flow for assigning the job to STQC for evaluation of CCTV as per ER by BIS" describes: BIS receives a request, forwards it to STQC, STQC assigns a lab, the lab tests and reports, STQC sends the report to BIS, and **BIS issues the certificate based on the STQC reports**. STQC's procedure document "P01 — Procedure for CCTV Testing Evaluation and Certification" (21 May 2024) describes testing CCTV cameras against the Essential Requirements under its IoT System Certification Scheme.

An earlier version of this article said STQC ran a separate certification "used mainly for government purchases". We could not support that from STQC's own documents and have removed it. For a buyer, the practical point is simpler: whatever role STQC played in testing, the public check is BIS's list.

## The model-certificate checklist (the second asset)

Use it on every quotation line that is a camera.

1. **Exact model string.** Copy it from the quotation, including suffixes (for example, a "-L" or "-Z" variant). A similar model in the same series is not the same model.
2. **R-number.** Ask the supplier for the BIS registration number of that model.
3. **Find it on the BIS list.** BIS publishes a "List of Registrations and models of CCTVs complying with ER for Security", with columns for R-number, manufacturer, brand, status, validity, "Models Complying with Security ER" and "Analog CCTV Camera Models".
4. **Status and validity.** The list shows each registration as Registered or Expired, with a validity date. On 8 October 2026 some entries showed Expired. Check the date has not passed.
5. **Right column.** A network (IP) camera should appear under "Models Complying with Security ER", not only under "Analog CCTV Camera Models".
6. **Brand is not compatibility, and not compliance.** Two models from the same brand can sit on different registrations, or one may not be listed.
7. **Keep evidence.** Save or print the list entry with the date you checked it, and attach it to the purchase order.
8. **Recorders.** The ER entry names "CCTV Camera". If a DVR or NVR is on the quotation, ask the supplier which CRO entry and registration it falls under, and check it the same way. <!-- UNRESOLVED: DVR/NVR coverage under the CRO not checked. -->

**Claims not to accept on their own:** a "BIS ER" or "STQC" logo on a brochure, a certificate image sent on WhatsApp, "sir, sab certified hai", or a test report for a different model.

## What this means for a site that already has cameras

Mostly, more room than you are being told. If cameras are working and point at the right things, the sensible order is to check what they can do before spending on replacements — [whether to reuse or replace](/insights/reuse-existing-cctv-or-replace). Most sites are not short of cameras; they are short of anyone watching at the moment it matters. When a camera genuinely needs replacing, buy a listed model.

If you are adding analytics to existing cameras, the camera's model, firmware and stream decide compatibility, not the brand — [ONVIF and compatibility checks](/features/guides/onvif-integration) explains what to test. PGAK reuses compatible cameras a site already owns; suitability, stream access and any processing hardware are confirmed at assessment and itemised in the quote.

## Limitations

This article covers the CCTV entry in the Compulsory Registration Order. It does not cover government tender conditions, sector rules (banks, critical infrastructure) or any security standards your customers impose, which may be stricter. We could not verify the 16 January 2026 memorandum, so do not treat 1 April 2026 as an officially confirmed date until you have seen it.
