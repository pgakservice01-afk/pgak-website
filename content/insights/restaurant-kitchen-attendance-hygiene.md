**Straight answer: the fix for restaurant staff attendance is a camera at the staff entrance doing face recognition, not a shared fingerprint pad. Nobody touches a surface, so there's nothing to wipe down between shifts and nothing that misreads because someone's hands are wet, floury or gloved. Place it away from the stove — heat and steam are the real limit, not the technology.**

Walk into most restaurant kitchens in Punjab and the attendance device is a fingerprint scanner mounted just inside the back door. Every cook, every helper, every dishwasher presses the same pad, often with the same hands they were just using to portion raw chicken or knead dough. Nobody designed it this way on purpose — biometric attendance rolled out across industries as one product, and kitchens got the same box as offices.

It causes two separate problems. One is hygiene, which any FSSAI-conscious owner already feels uneasy about even without a formal audit flagging it. The other is accuracy: grease and flour on fingertips are exactly the conditions fingerprint sensors handle worst, so misreads climb during service hours, and misreads turn into manual entries — which is where attendance records quietly stop meaning anything.

## Why does a fingerprint scanner fail more in a kitchen than an office?

Fingerprint sensors read the ridges on skin optically or capacitively, and both methods need a reasonably clean, dry finger to get a clean match. Kitchen staff arrive with wet hands from washing up, oily hands from frying, or flour-dusted hands from the tandoor section. Each of those degrades the read. The usual fix — pressing harder, trying a different finger, wiping the pad on a kitchen towel that was just used for something else — doesn't solve the hygiene issue, it compounds it.

## What replaces the shared pad?

A camera doing face recognition at the staff entry, logging each entry and exit against a timestamp with no surface anyone touches. It removes the biometric hygiene question entirely, because there's nothing to press. It also removes the failure mode of wet or dirty hands, since a face doesn't change texture depending on what someone was just cooking.

We build systems like this, so weigh that disclosure accordingly. The honest limitation: placement matters more here than in most settings. Right above a fryer or next to a tandoor, steam and heat haze will degrade a camera's read the same way grease degrades a fingerprint sensor — different mechanism, same outcome. The fix is straightforward: put the camera at the staff entry door itself, before anyone reaches the cooking line, not inside the kitchen proper.

## How should restaurants handle split shifts?

Food and beverage staffing runs on lunch and dinner blocks with a gap in between, and a lot of attendance systems assume one continuous shift per day. If the system doesn't have a split-shift setting, a staff member who leaves at 3 PM and returns at 6 PM either shows as absent for three hours they were never scheduled to work, or as one long shift that overstates their hours. Define lunch and dinner as two shift blocks from the start. It's a five-minute configuration change that prevents a recurring payroll argument.

## What about staff who rotate between outlets?

Multi-outlet restaurant groups often move staff between locations for a busy weekend or a colleague's leave. If each outlet's attendance system is a closed island, that staff member's hours from the second location either don't get counted or get counted twice against two separate payroll runs. This needs a shared staff list across outlets, not a shared device — the fix is organisational as much as technical, and no camera solves it on its own.

## What this doesn't fix

A touch-free entry system stops the hygiene problem and the misread problem. It doesn't stop a manager who's willing to mark someone present who genuinely isn't there — that's a process control, not a technology one. And it won't help if the entry point itself isn't enforced; staff who can walk in through a side door that has no camera will simply use it. The camera only works as well as the chokepoint it's watching.
