A restaurant needs attendance records that make sense across preparation, lunch service, the gap between services and evening work. Buying a touch-free device does not resolve that schedule by itself. The entry point, staff assignments and exception process need to be designed together.

Camera-based attendance can avoid a particular shared contact surface when the proposed workflow supports it. That is not a claim that it removes infection risk, replaces cleaning or makes a kitchen compliant. Keep the restaurant's approved food-safety and hygiene procedures in place regardless of the attendance method.

## Assess the staff route before the device

Map where employees normally enter and leave, including deliveries, temporary access restrictions and alternative doors. Check whether the proposed view can observe the intended attendance event without placing equipment in the way of work or required cleaning. Do not assume a camera pointed at a cooking area is an attendance camera.

Test normal lighting and movement at the proposed position. Check the actual image for obstructions and maintenance needs. An attractive still photograph from an empty entrance is not a representative test of staff arriving together before service.

Keep required masks, hair coverings, gloves and other hygiene or safety equipment in place wherever the site's procedures require them. Do not create an attendance routine that asks employees to remove required equipment in a food-handling area. The [PPE and headwear guide](/insights/masks-helmets-turbans-face-recognition) explains how to scope a realistic trial and fallback.

## Represent split shifts explicitly

Ask the roster owner to explain the intended work blocks and breaks. A person leaving between lunch and dinner services should not be treated as continuously working merely because the first entry and last exit are far apart. Equally, a planned gap should not silently become an unexplained absence.

Confirm how the selected software represents split duties, multiple entries and approved changes. Use the actual roster and export needed by the restaurant. If the system cannot represent the required arrangement directly, document the supported review process rather than describing the setup as a quick universal setting.

[Zoho People's shift documentation](https://www.zoho.com/people/api/attendance-shift-details.html) illustrates that employee assignments and schedule details are separate information from attendance capture. It is an external product example, not evidence that a PGAK installation includes restaurant rostering or that every split-shift pattern is supported.

## Keep outlet transfers attached to the right person

Where staff cover another outlet, use a stable employee ID and an effective assignment. Decide which supervisor reviews that day's exceptions and which team approves the final report. Creating a second identity at the temporary outlet can split the record and complicate reconciliation.

Do not infer an outlet transfer simply because a camera recorded a familiar face. The approved assignment and the observed event should remain separate. The [multi-location attendance guide](/insights/multi-location-attendance-management) can support a wider rollout; begin with a small representative transfer before scaling.

## Plan exceptions during a busy service

Name a reviewer and backup for missed events or failed recognition. Give staff a practical way to report the issue without blocking the entrance or interrupting a safety-critical task. Keep the reason, original observation and approved correction together.

Test what happens during a network or device outage. Confirm whether events are retained, when they become available and how a controlled fallback reaches the report. Avoid claiming that a device works offline unless the exact proposed configuration has been demonstrated.

During a trial, review unresolved records, correction time and whether the setup disrupted normal hygiene or staff movement. These are operational measures. They do not establish health outcomes, automatic payroll accuracy or a universal best method for every kitchen.

## Common questions

### Does touch-free attendance replace kitchen hygiene procedures?

No. It may avoid a specific shared touchpoint, but cleaning, hand hygiene and other approved food-safety controls remain separate responsibilities. Attendance equipment does not establish compliance by itself.

### Should the first entry and last exit be treated as one working interval?

Not automatically. Use the approved roster and break or split-shift arrangements, then review the relevant events and exceptions. Presence at the site is not the same as approved working time.
