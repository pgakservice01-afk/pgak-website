## Your cameras are some years old

You put up your CCTV a few years ago. It still records. But the picture looks softer than before, and one camera drops off now and then. Is it time to replace it, or can it keep working?

There is no clock that tells you when a camera is finished. A camera does not usually stop on a set date. It wears slowly. This guide helps you read that wear and make a calm decision.

## Age is a weak guide

You will find many articles that quote a number of years. Treat those as rough talk from sellers and installers, not as a rule. Two cameras bought the same day can age very differently. One sits under a shaded porch. The other faces west on a hot wall and gets splashed in the monsoon.

So ask about the camera's condition, not just its birthday.

## What shortens a camera's life

- **Heat.** A camera in direct summer sun or a closed box runs hot. Heat ages electronics and plastic.
- **Damp and dust.** Water in a housing, corroded connectors and dust on the lens damage the picture. See [CCTV monsoon failures](/insights/cctv-monsoon-failures).
- **Power quality.** Surges, weak adapters and long cables stress a camera. A poor supply can fail before the camera does. [The CCTV power backup checklist](/insights/cctv-power-backup-checklist) covers this.
- **Moving parts.** A camera that pans and tilts has motors and gears that wear. A fixed camera has fewer parts that move.
- **Missing upkeep.** A camera that no one cleans or checks fades faster. [The CCTV maintenance checklist for business](/insights/cctv-maintenance-checklist-for-business) is a simple routine.

## Signs a camera is getting old

Look at the live picture and walk the site. Watch for:

- a hazy, dull or yellowish image that cleaning does not fix
- weak night vision, or a ring of light with a dark edge
- water marks or fog inside the dome
- a cracked or faded housing
- colours that look wrong
- a camera that drops off the recorder, which is called video loss

Compare the picture now with an image you saved when the camera was new. If you do not have one, save a fresh image today for next time.

## Is the picture still good enough for the job?

A camera is useful if it still does the job you bought it for. Ask:

- Can you read a vehicle number plate or recognise the area you need?
- Does night footage show enough to be useful? [How to test CCTV night vision](/insights/how-to-test-cctv-night-vision) gives a simple test.
- Does it stay online all day?

If the answer is yes, the camera may still be doing its work. If the picture no longer shows what you need, replacing it is reasonable.

## Keep, repair or replace?

A rough way to decide:

- **Keep** a camera whose picture is clear and which stays online.
- **Repair** a camera with a fixable fault, such as a bad connector, a dirty lens or a weak adapter.
- **Replace** a camera whose picture is poor, whose housing has failed, or whose repair costs approach a new one.

The right answer also depends on the recorder, cabling and what you want from the system. [Reuse existing CCTV or replace it](/insights/reuse-existing-cctv-or-replace) walks through the trade-offs, and [the retrofit vs replacement calculator](/calculators/retrofit-vs-replacement) helps you compare costs for your own numbers.

## When an old camera can still work with AI

Many people want video analytics but worry they must throw out all their cameras. That is not always true. What matters is picture quality, the video format, the angle and whether the camera can send its stream to the software. [Adding AI to existing CCTV cameras](/insights/add-ai-to-existing-cctv-cameras) explains what to check, and the [compatibility page](/platform/compatibility) lists how PGAK approaches it.

A camera that is old but has a clear picture and a good angle may be a better starting point than a new camera in a poor position. The way to find out is to test a sample of the real feed. No one can promise a result from a camera's age alone.

## A simple plan

1. Save a current image from every camera.
2. Note which cameras drop out or look hazy.
3. Fix cheap faults first: clean, reseat, replace weak adapters.
4. Replace only the cameras that no longer do their job.
5. If you want AI analytics, test your existing feeds before you buy new hardware.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a CCTV camera last?

There is no single answer. Installers and makers quote a range of years, but real life depends on where the camera is, its build, heat, damp, power quality and upkeep. Judge each camera by its picture and condition, not by its age alone.

### How do I know my CCTV camera is getting old?

Look for a hazy or dim picture, weak night vision, colour shifts, water marks inside the dome, a cracked housing, or a camera that drops off the recorder. Compare today's picture with a saved image from when it was new.

### Can an old CCTV camera work with AI analytics?

Sometimes. It depends on picture quality, the camera's video format and whether it can reach the analytics software. Test a sample of the actual feed through a feasibility check before deciding to keep or replace it.
