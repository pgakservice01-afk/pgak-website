## The price on the first quote is rarely the full cost

A school office gets three CCTV quotes. One is much lower. Then the questions begin: does it include cabling, a recorder, storage, installation and upkeep? Does it cover the gate, corridors and playground? Who watches the footage?

A school budget needs to answer those questions before approval. This checklist helps a principal, trustee or administrator compare quotes on the same footing.

## 1. Start with the rules that apply to you

Boards and state authorities may set expectations for school cameras, including where they must be placed, what they should record and how long footage is kept. These rules change, so do not rely on a supplier's summary.

Ask your board or education department for the current circular. Share it with every supplier in writing, and ask each one to say how the quote meets it. Keep a copy with the purchase file.

## 2. Decide which areas matter most

You may not be able to cover everything at once. List the areas in order: main gate, other entrances and exits, parking, corridors, staircases, labs, playground, office and cash counter. Then mark which are required by the rules and which are your own priority.

A phased plan is a fair approach: cover the required and highest-risk areas first, and add the rest in the next budget. Ask each supplier to quote by area so you can see where the money goes. For the gate itself, [our school security page](/school-security) shows how PGAK approaches gates and entrances.

## 3. Ask for a complete cost, not a per-camera price

A quote should list these as separate lines:

- cameras, with the model and the number per area;
- recorder and storage;
- cabling and power;
- installation and commissioning;
- staff training;
- maintenance and repair after handover.

If an item is missing, ask who pays for it. A low camera price with no cabling line often means extra charges later. [CCTV total cost of ownership over five years](/insights/cctv-total-cost-of-ownership-5-years) explains how to compare costs over time.

## 4. Check storage and how long footage is kept

Storage depends on the number of cameras, picture quality and the days of footage you want to keep. Ask each supplier how many days of footage their quote stores, at what settings. ## 5. Look at the building and cabling

Older school buildings can make cabling harder: thick walls, long corridors, separate blocks. A site visit matters. Ask the supplier to walk the campus and mark camera positions and cable routes before they quote. A quote prepared without a visit may change after installation starts.

## 6. Decide who watches, who has access and who maintains

Cameras need someone responsible for them. Ask who checks that the system is recording, who can view and export footage, and who is called when a camera fails. Write the roles down. Add a monthly check to the maintenance plan. [How do you know when a CCTV camera stops recording?](/insights/cctv-camera-offline-how-to-know) describes simple checks.

## 7. Tell parents and staff what the cameras do

Plan a clear notice and a policy on who can request footage. [CCTV signage in India](/insights/cctv-signage-requirements-india) lists what a notice should explain. Doing this before installation avoids confusion later.

## 8. Ask about funding, but verify it

Some schools can use trust funds, management budgets or state and local schemes. Ask your education department or board whether any support applies to you. Do not rely on a supplier's word about a scheme; ask for the official notice.

## Where AI analytics fits, and where it does not

Most schools need working cameras, enough storage and a clear process first. AI video analytics may help with a narrow, defined need, such as an alert at a gate after hours, but it depends on a usable view from a compatible camera and on a response plan. It does not replace staff.

If you already have cameras, a feasibility check can show whether a defined event could be flagged from that feed. [PGAK's pricing approach](/pricing) starts from existing cameras instead of assuming a new system.

## Quick approval checklist

- Current board or state circular collected and shared with suppliers.
- Areas listed in priority order, with phases.
- Quotes broken into cameras, recorder, storage, cabling, installation, training and upkeep.
- Days of stored footage stated and checked.
- Site visit done before the final quote.
- Roles for viewing, export and maintenance written down.
- Notice and footage-request policy ready.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a school CCTV quote include?

A clear school CCTV quote lists cameras, recorder and storage, cabling, installation, commissioning, training and a maintenance plan as separate lines. If one line is missing, ask who pays for it and when. Ask for the camera count per area so you can check coverage.

### Do schools need to follow board rules for CCTV?

Rules differ by board and state and can change. Ask your board or education department for the current circular and share it with every supplier. A quote should say how it meets those requirements, area by area.

### Is cheaper CCTV always worse for a school?

Not always, but a low price can hide missing items such as storage, cabling or upkeep. Compare complete project costs on the same coverage and the same number of days of stored footage, not the per-camera price alone.
