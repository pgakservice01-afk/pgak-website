An employee leaves the site after the scheduled finish. The attendance event may be correct, yet it does not explain whether the person was working, waiting for transport or completing an authorized handover. Conversely, a missing exit does not prove that no extra work happened. Overtime review needs more than a final timestamp.

Keep observed presence, reported work, authorization and payroll treatment as separate fields or linked records. This gives the responsible reviewer an evidence trail without asking a camera or attendance application to make a wage decision it cannot justify.

## Gather the records before debating the total

For the disputed period, collect the relevant roster version, original entry and exit observations, work or supervisor records and any prior corrections. Use stable employee and site identifiers. Check whether a transfer or shift swap changed the schedule against which the report was calculated.

Record the source of each item. A supervisor's later confirmation is different from an automatically recorded event; both can be useful when their origin is clear. Do not rewrite the event history to make the final summary appear uninterrupted.

The authorized HR and payroll team should determine the applicable treatment with appropriate employment-law advice. Prior-approval rules alone do not decide every payment obligation. Avoid assuming that unapproved time can automatically be ignored, reduced or withheld.

## Look for transformations between source and summary

Ask which time the system stores and which time it displays. A rounded summary, duplicate-event filter or delayed upload can make the final report look different from the original observations. Have the supplier explain those transformations with a test case, then retain enough detail for a reviewer to reconstruct the result.

Do not choose a rounding method simply because it makes disputes easier to close. The responsible advisers should approve any applicable calculation rule. Test that the configured method matches that approval and that later changes are versioned.

Check overnight work across calendar boundaries. The same event sequence should remain understandable in the [shift-attendance report](/insights/shift-management-attendance-india) and the payroll handoff. A date label that changes between exports should not silently create or remove time.

## Handle missing events without guessing

Flag a missing entry or exit for review. Replacing it automatically with the scheduled time can conceal uncertainty. If a supported rule proposes an estimate, label it as such and require the appropriate review before it becomes an approved record.

Use available, authorized evidence relevant to the specific case: a work-order record, supervisor confirmation or suitable camera observation. A gate image may indicate presence at that gate; it does not establish continuous work throughout the interval. Keep the evidence reference restricted to people who need it for the review.

Give unresolved cases an owner and next checkpoint. The point is to resolve them while useful information remains available, not to declare that every discrepancy can be settled on the same day.

## Demonstrate the approval handoff

Use a sample with ordinary hours, a reported extension, an overnight shift and a corrected event. Compare the raw observations, reviewed attendance and export. Check that the receiving system can distinguish an approved result from an unresolved item and a revised result from a duplicate.

[Zoho People's attendance documentation](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/reports/articles/attendance-reports) describes separate presence and payable-hours reporting. This is a product-specific example of distinctions worth examining, not confirmation of PGAK payroll calculation features.

The [payroll integration guide](/insights/biometric-attendance-payroll-integration) provides a broader acceptance checklist. Require an actual demonstration of the intended export or integration, with responsibilities documented on both sides. A spreadsheet that opens successfully is not enough if employee IDs or correction flags are lost.

## Common questions

### Does staying after the shift automatically establish overtime pay?

An attendance timestamp alone does not decide payable time. Preserve the observation and refer the work, authorization and applicable requirements to the responsible HR and payroll review process.

### Should a missing exit be replaced with the scheduled finish?

Not silently. Keep the gap visible and use the approved correction process. Any proposed estimate should be identifiable and reviewed rather than presented as an original observation.
