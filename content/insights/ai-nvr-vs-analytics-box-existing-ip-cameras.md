## You already own the cameras. Now what?

A factory owner, a CCTV dealer or a warehouse manager often says the same thing: "My cameras are fine. I only want alerts and search on top." Then three kinds of product are offered, and each salesperson says theirs is the easy one.

This guide explains the three common routes in plain words, so you can pick the one that fits your site rather than the one that is pushed hardest.

## Route 1: an AI NVR

An AI NVR is a network video recorder with analytics built in. You connect your IP cameras to it, and it records and runs detection in one box.

**Where it fits:** a small site, a handful of cameras, and an old recorder that is due for replacement anyway.

**What to watch:**
- The analytics available are those the maker built in. You cannot add a new rule type later unless the maker ships it.
- Your current recorder is replaced, so the footage history on it needs a plan.
- Check which of your camera brands and models it can actually connect to.

## Route 2: a separate analytics box beside your recorder

Here the old DVR or NVR keeps recording. A separate unit reads the camera streams and runs the detection. Alerts and events are kept next to the footage.

**Where it fits:** you want to keep your recorder, or you have a mix of camera brands that no single AI NVR supports.

**What to watch:**
- Each camera gives a second stream to the box. Cameras and the network must handle that extra load.
- Two devices means two things that can fail. Ask who is responsible when one stops.
- Confirm how the box reaches the cameras: directly, or through the recorder.

## Route 3: a server-based setup for many cameras

When there are many cameras, or several sites, the analytics run on a server. This can be on your premises or hosted. The cameras and recorders stay where they are.

**Where it fits:** multiple sites, many cameras, or a business that wants one place to see events. See [how multi-site monitoring works](/platform/deployment) for the general approach.

**What to watch:**
- Bandwidth. Moving video to a remote server needs a stable connection. Use the [bandwidth and cloud cost calculator](/calculators/bandwidth-and-cloud-cost) to size it with your own figures.
- What happens when the internet drops. Read [whether AI CCTV works without internet](/insights/does-ai-cctv-work-without-internet).

## Questions that matter more than the product type

The box is the easy part. These questions decide whether it works:

1. **Can it read my cameras?** Ask for the list of supported protocols. ONVIF is a common standard for IP camera interoperability, and the [ONVIF site](https://www.onvif.org/) explains what its profiles cover. A label is not proof, so ask for a test on your own feed.
2. **Is the camera view suitable?** Analytics depends on angle, distance and light. A camera that looks fine to a person can still be poor for detection.
3. **Where do alerts go?** Ask who receives them, on which device, and what a person does next.
4. **How are false alerts handled?** Read [how to reduce AI CCTV false alarms](/insights/ai-cctv-false-alarms-how-to-reduce) and ask the seller to describe their process.
5. **What does the recorder keep?** Detection does not replace reliable recording. Check [storage needs](/calculators/cctv-storage) for your camera count.
6. **Who supports it?** Ask who answers when a camera goes offline, and how fast.

## A simple way to decide

- **One small site, old recorder, few cameras:** an AI NVR may be enough.
- **Good recorder, mixed cameras, want to keep it:** a separate analytics unit.
- **Many cameras or several sites:** a server-based setup.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to replace my IP cameras to get AI features?
Not always. If the cameras give a usable video stream and the view suits the task, analytics can often run on the existing feed. Camera angle, light and stream access matter more than the brand, so test a sample feed first.

### Is an AI NVR the same as an analytics box?
No. An AI NVR is a recorder with analytics built in, so recording and detection sit in one unit. An analytics box sits beside your current recorder, reads the camera streams and adds detection without replacing the recorder.

### Can I keep my existing recorder and still get AI alerts?
Often yes. A separate analytics unit or server can read the camera streams directly while your DVR or NVR keeps recording. The recorder still needs to be reliable, so check its health and storage first.
