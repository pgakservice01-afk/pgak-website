Video analytics software analyses video for defined events or patterns. In a business CCTV system, that can support a workflow in which an event is flagged for a person to review and act on.

The useful buying question is specific: which observable event should receive attention at your site? “Make our CCTV intelligent” does not define the camera view, event rule, recipient or response you need.

## Separate recording, detection and response

A recorder preserves video according to its configuration. Analytics processes an available stream or recording for a supported purpose. An event-delivery system routes a result. A person or agreed operating process then decides what action is appropriate.

These layers need testing together. A detection that never reaches the right person does not complete the workflow. An alert also does not establish intent or prove misconduct; its context still needs review.

For example, a warehouse may want to flag a person entering a closed loading bay after the agreed shift. That is an illustrative requirement. It needs a suitable view, operating schedule, permitted exceptions and a responder, as well as software that supports the event.

## How is it different from motion detection?

Basic motion detection reacts to changes in an image. Some analytics applications add object classification or rules for particular areas, lines or time conditions. The exact functions depend on the product and configuration.

Do not assume that the word “AI” removes unwanted alerts. Lighting, obstructions, image quality and scene changes can still affect results. Ask a supplier to show both the events you want and the ordinary activity you want handled appropriately.

If you already have too many alerts, use the [false-alert review checklist](/insights/ai-cctv-false-alarms-how-to-reduce) before deciding that a new product alone will solve the problem.

## Where does the software run?

Analytics may run within a supported camera, on equipment at the site, in a hosted service or across a combination. Ask which arrangement is proposed rather than assuming one model applies to every installation.

| Question | Why it matters to the purchase |
| --- | --- |
| Which component processes video? | Identifies equipment, support and capacity requirements. |
| What video leaves the site? | Clarifies access, bandwidth and handling arrangements. |
| What stops during an interruption? | Separates recording, analysis, remote viewing and message delivery. |
| How are channels and features licensed? | Makes expansion and renewal costs visible. |
| Who maintains the configuration? | Establishes ownership after commissioning. |

Request a diagram of the proposed video and alert path. A claim that a system “works offline” needs to specify exactly which functions remain available and under which conditions.

## Can it use your existing cameras?

Possibly, but approval must apply to the tested combination. Record the camera or recorder model, firmware, stream settings and proposed use. Then check both access to supported video and the quality of the view.

A feed can be readable but unsuitable for a particular event. A camera's age alone neither proves nor disproves suitability. PGAK's [compatibility checklist](/platform/compatibility) explains what to prepare without sending camera passwords or unrestricted access links.

## Buy against a test, not an accuracy slogan

Agree the event definition and acceptable results before a pilot. Keep an independent log of eligible events, correct alerts, false alerts, missed events, delivery delay and interruptions. Include the relevant operating conditions and state what was not tested.

Use the [evaluation worksheet](/resources/evaluation-method) to compare candidates consistently. A confidence score shown by software is not the same as measured accuracy at your site. Small staged samples also do not establish performance across all shifts, seasons or locations.

If the pilot exposes a problem, record whether the next step is a different view, a configuration change, another test or stopping the proposal. A useful evaluation can conclude that the current setup is unsuitable.

## Define the first purchase clearly

Start with one business problem, a manageable set of relevant views and a named response process. Request an itemised scope covering integration, equipment, licences, commissioning and support. Expand only after the intended workflow has been demonstrated.
