A camera that works in dry weather may develop an unusable image or lose connectivity during rain. Before the next wet period, inspect the installation as a system: camera housing, connections, mounting, power, network and recording.

Maintenance can reduce avoidable faults. It cannot promise that a short inspection prevents every storm-related failure. Keep a record of defects, repairs and the functions that were actually tested.

## Confirm what is installed outdoors

Record the camera model and the manufacturer's environmental limits. Check whether the housing, accessories, cable entry and installation method are suitable for the location. An enclosure rating is not a guarantee for a damaged seal or an incorrectly assembled connection.

Ask a competent technician to inspect elevated equipment, electrical protection and any enclosure that must be opened. A walk-around can identify visible concerns; it is not a reason to handle wet electrical equipment or dismantle cameras without the proper procedure.

Use the [maintenance-contract checklist](/insights/cctv-amc-what-should-it-include) to confirm who owns inspection, repair and replacement work.

## Inspect the water path, not only the camera body

Look for damaged glands, loose covers, exposed connectors, deteriorated seals and cable routing that brings water towards an entry point. Record the location and arrange correction using the manufacturer's specified parts and method.

Cisco Meraki's [outdoor camera gland instructions](https://documentation.meraki.com/IoT/MV_-_Smart_Cameras/Install_and_Get_Started/MV44X_Installation_Guide/MV_Outdoor_Series_Waterproof_Gland_Installation) illustrate why the connection assembly matters. They describe the product's exposure limits and require the gland to be sealed correctly. Apply the instructions for your own equipment, rather than copying a different camera's assembly details.

Do not assume that tape, additional sealant or a makeshift cover is an approved repair. An unsuitable treatment may interfere with drainage, ventilation, warranty or later servicing.

## Investigate a fogged or obscured image

Record whether moisture or dirt appears outside the cover or within the housing, when the problem occurs and whether it clears. Condensation, contamination, damaged seals and other installation conditions need investigation; an image alone may not establish the cause.

Follow the manufacturer's cleaning and servicing instructions. Avoid opening a housing in wet conditions or removing internal moisture-control parts without guidance. After the work, inspect the required day and night views, not only the camera's connection status.

Rain on the cover, glare and moving objects near a lens can also affect the useful scene. A camera being online does not prove that its recording or analytics remains suitable for the intended task.

## Size backup for the complete required path

List the equipment needed during an outage: cameras or their power supplies, PoE switches, recorder, processing device and network equipment where remote delivery is required. Backing up only the recorder may leave other necessary components unpowered.

Ask for the actual load and the selected UPS manufacturer's runtime information. A VA rating alone is not a duration promise. Schneider Electric's [APC battery guidance](https://www.se.com/us/en/faqs/FA158934/) explains that increasing load reduces runtime and that battery condition matters.

Agree a safe test with the site owner and technician. Follow the equipment's test procedure rather than repeatedly draining batteries. Keep the load, test conditions, observed behaviour and any unverified duration clear in the record.

## Verify recovery after an interruption

| Function | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- |
| Camera picture | Expected view restored, current time and usable image |
| Recording | Recent playback available on required channels |
| Analytics, if installed | Intended rules active on supported streams |
| Notifications, if installed | Agreed test received by the responsible person |
| Remaining faults | Named owner, next checkpoint and temporary coverage |

Use the [camera-health guide](/insights/cctv-camera-offline-how-to-know) to separate these checks. An automatic restart does not prove that every service resumed correctly.
