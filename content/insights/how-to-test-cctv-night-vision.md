## A bright night image can still be unusable

Many CCTV systems look impressive on a live monitor after dark. The important question is narrower: can you see useful detail at the gate, shutter, yard line or loading bay where an event would happen?

A camera may light up the wall next to it while leaving the approach path dark. It may also show a person as a silhouette if a bright light sits behind them. Test the scene, not the specification sheet.

## Plan a short after-dark walkthrough

Pick the priority views: main gate, staff entrance, loading bay, boundary line, cash counter or stock-room door. Check them after dark at the times they are actually used. If the site has shift changes, test during one.

Have a designated person walk through the relevant path only when it is safe and authorised. The purpose is to inspect usable footage, not to stage a security incident.

## The CCTV night-vision checklist

### 1. Watch the point of approach

Do not only stand below the camera. Walk from the direction a visitor, vehicle or intruder would approach. At a gate, that is often several metres before the entrance—not the area directly under the light.

### 2. Check facial and clothing detail where it matters

For a general overview camera, a broad scene may be enough. For an entry point where identification is required, the camera needs a more suitable angle and distance. A wide view cannot be assumed to provide identity-level detail.

[Camera placement for AI detection](/insights/where-to-place-cctv-cameras-for-ai-detection) explains why overview and identity views often need different placement.

### 3. Look for backlight and glare

Vehicle headlights, floodlights, a bright shutter opening or a lamp pointed towards the lens can reduce usable detail. Move or shield a light only with the appropriate site approval; a lighting change can affect safety as well as video.

### 4. Check infrared reflection

Infrared can bounce from glass, white walls, nearby signs, cobwebs or rain. The result may be a bright haze close to the camera and a dark scene beyond it. Clean the exterior surface carefully according to the equipment guidance and investigate persistent reflection.

### 5. Check the image at the full distance you care about

A camera's advertised infrared range is not the same as the distance at which your scene produces useful evidence. Check the far end of the bay, edge of the yard or actual gate line.

### 6. Review the recording, not only the live feed

After the walkthrough, play back the time window from the NVR or DVR. Compression, recording settings or dropped frames can make playback worse than the live image.

### 7. Check the view in difficult weather and power conditions

Rain, fog, dust and a power event can change the usable image. You do not need to manufacture unsafe conditions; record observations when real conditions occur and include them in the maintenance log.

## Where AI analytics fits—and where it does not

AI video analytics needs a stable, usable camera view. It can help flag a defined event from a compatible feed, but it cannot restore detail that was never captured because a subject is too far away, backlit or obscured.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if CCTV night vision is good enough?

Test the actual scene after dark and review the recorded playback. The useful question is whether the footage shows enough detail at the distance and point where an event is likely to occur, not whether the image merely looks bright.

### Why is my CCTV image blurry or washed out at night?

Common causes include dirty lenses, infrared reflection from glass or a nearby wall, bright lights behind a subject, poor focus, insufficient scene lighting or an unsuitable camera angle.
