## CCTV access should be purposeful, not casual

When a workplace has CCTV, the question is not simply whether somebody can log in. It is whether they have a defined reason to view a specific feed or recording.

Unrestricted access creates avoidable risk: footage can be viewed for the wrong reason, copied without approval or shared outside the incident process. At the same time, locking the system away from everyone can slow a legitimate response.

The workable middle ground is a simple access policy: give the right people the right level of access for a documented purpose.

## Start with roles, not a long list of names

Most sites need fewer access levels than they think. A practical model may include:

- **System administrator:** manages user accounts, passwords, devices and configuration. This role should not automatically investigate every incident.
- **Security or operations reviewer:** can view live or recorded footage relevant to their responsibility.
- **Incident approver:** authorises exports or wider access when an incident, safety review, complaint or lawful request requires it.
- **Temporary viewer:** receives time-limited or area-limited access for a specific task, if the platform supports it.

Keep a current list of the people assigned to each role. Remove access promptly when responsibilities change.

## Decide what each role can do

“Can view CCTV” is too broad. Make the permission specific:

- Live-view access only, or playback too?
- All cameras, or only the relevant site, gate or department?
- Can the person download clips or only view them?
- Can the person change recording settings, camera positions or user accounts?
- Is access available all the time or only during an incident?

Exporting footage deserves extra control because an exported file can be copied, forwarded or stored outside the original system. Use an approval step and a record of why the export was made.

## Keep a simple access and incident log

A site does not need an overcomplicated process to be accountable. A lightweight register can include:

- Date and time of review
- Person who accessed the system
- Reason for access
- Camera area and time window reviewed
- Whether a clip was exported
- Approval reference, where required
- Where the exported clip is stored and when it should be deleted or preserved

Choose a system that can also retain technical audit logs when possible. The written incident record adds context that a login record cannot.

## Be transparent with employees and visitors

CCTV can serve genuine safety, security and operational needs. Still, people should not be surprised by monitoring. Use clear signage where appropriate and align camera locations, purpose, access and retention with the organisation's policy and applicable requirements.

This article is practical guidance, not legal advice. Workplace CCTV and employee-data obligations can depend on the location, purpose, information captured and how it is used. For high-risk or sensitive deployments, obtain suitable legal or privacy advice.

## Where video analytics fits

AI video analytics can surface defined events from compatible CCTV cameras, such as entry into a restricted zone. It should not become a reason to give more people unrestricted access to every feed.

For each alert workflow, decide:

1. Who receives the alert?
2. Who reviews the related video?
3. What action is permitted?
4. Who can export evidence?
5. How long should the material be retained?
