## You asked for wireless, and the quote still lists cables

You search for a wireless CCTV camera with night vision and a recorder. The listings look simple. Then the installer says you still need power points, and a Wi-Fi router near the camera. That is normal. The word "wireless" is used loosely, and it helps to know what it covers before you pay.

## What wireless usually means

In most listings, wireless means the **video** reaches the recorder or your phone over Wi-Fi or a radio link instead of a cable. It rarely means the camera needs no power. Each camera still needs one of these:

- a power cable and adapter from a nearby socket;
- a battery that must be charged;
- a solar panel with a battery.

So a wireless camera is not a camera you can stick up anywhere. It must sit within reach of power and of a usable signal. If power is the real problem, [this guide to PoE power budgets](/insights/poe-switch-cctv-camera-power-budget) explains the wired option that carries power and video in one cable.

## Wi-Fi camera, wireless kit or IP camera?

Three different products are sold under the same word:

- **Wi-Fi camera.** It joins your router and usually records to a memory card or a cloud plan. It is simple, but it depends on the router and the signal.
- **Wireless kit.** Cameras send video to a matched recorder over a radio link. The recorder and cameras are meant to be used together.
- **IP camera on a network recorder.** It may use Wi-Fi or a cable. If you later want AI analytics, check whether the camera follows the standard way of connecting. ONVIF support alone does not mean every AI function will work, so ask the supplier to test your exact camera model.

Ask which of the three you are being quoted.

## Check the signal where the camera will actually sit

Wireless range quoted on a box is measured in the open. Walls, steel, water tanks and distance cut it down. A camera on a first-floor wall behind a brick room may not hold a steady link.

Test before you drill. Mount the camera temporarily at the planned height, view the live picture on the recorder or phone, and also play back a recording. A choppy picture or dropped frames mean the link is too weak.

## Check night vision separately

Night vision and wireless are two separate checks. Night picture depends on the infrared range, the lens, the lighting and reflections. A camera facing a glass door or white wall can show a bright haze. Use the steps in [How to test CCTV night vision](/insights/how-to-test-cctv-night-vision) after dark, at the distance that matters.

Advertised night range is a general figure. Check the picture where an event would happen, such as the gate line or counter, not just near the camera.

## Check the recorder, not only the camera

The recorder decides how long footage lasts and how easy it is to find. Before you buy, ask:

- How many cameras can it take, and does that include the cameras you plan to add later?
- How many days of footage will it hold at your video settings? Use the [CCTV storage calculator](/calculators/cctv-storage) to estimate.
- Can you play back a time window quickly, and export a clip?
- If it is a DVR or an NVR, which one? [DVR or NVR: identify your recorder](/insights/dvr-vs-nvr-which-do-you-have) helps you tell them apart.

## Plan for power cuts and security

A wireless camera that loses power or Wi-Fi stops recording. Ask what happens during a power cut, and whether the recorder sits on a backup. [CCTV power backup checklist](/insights/cctv-power-backup-checklist) covers the basics.

Wi-Fi cameras should have the default password changed, and the router and camera software kept up to date. Ask who will do this after installation.

## Where AI analytics fits, and where it does not

AI video analytics needs a stable, readable picture from a compatible camera. A weak Wi-Fi link, dropped frames or a washed-out night image will limit what any analytics can do. Fix the basics first: power, signal, night picture and recording.

If you already have cameras that cover a gate, stock area or counter, a feasibility check can show whether a defined event could be flagged from that feed. [PGAK's pricing approach](/pricing) starts from the cameras you have rather than assuming a full replacement.

## Short buying checklist

- Which type is it: Wi-Fi camera, wireless kit or IP camera?
- How is each camera powered, and is a socket close?
- Signal tested at the real mounting spot.
- Night picture tested after dark at the real distance.
- Recorder capacity, playback and export checked.
- Backup power and password plan agreed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a wireless CCTV camera need a power cable?

Usually yes. Wireless normally describes how the video travels, over Wi-Fi or a radio link. Most wireless cameras still need a power cable, a power adapter or a battery. Ask exactly how each camera is powered before you plan the install.

### Can a wireless camera record to a recorder?

Yes, many kits pair wireless cameras with a recorder, and some Wi-Fi cameras also record to a memory card. Check that the recorder supports your cameras, how many it can take, and how long the footage is kept.

### Is wireless CCTV reliable at night?

Night picture quality depends on infrared range, lens and lighting. Wireless signal depends on walls, distance and interference. They are separate checks, so test both after dark at the position where the camera will be fixed.
