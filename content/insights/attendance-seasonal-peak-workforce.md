A seasonal intake changes more than the number of names in an attendance system. New workers, temporary supervisors, unfamiliar entrances and a busy dispatch schedule can arrive together. Preparing only the camera or only the enrolment desk leaves other parts of the process untested.

Start with the actual expected workforce and arrival pattern. Do not assume that enrolment is always the only bottleneck, or that storage, software limits and camera processing remain unaffected. The assessment should cover the full route from registration to the attendance report someone approves.

## Build a usable roster before the busy period

Confirm who supplies the worker list and who checks it. Record a stable worker ID, the responsible employer or contractor where relevant, intended site, expected start date and supervisor. Avoid merging people merely because their names match or creating a fresh identity every time a returning worker appears.

Returning workers need a deliberate identity check and reactivation decision. Their previous record may contain an obsolete site assignment or employment status. Reuse information only through the organization's approved process; keeping a historic record does not automatically authorize a new biometric capture or current access.

For contractor-specific reconciliation, use the [contract-labour attendance guide](/insights/attendance-system-for-contract-labour). Keep the seasonal plan focused on volume, temporary assignments and the end of the engagement.

## Measure the setup and capture capacity

Time a representative registration from arrival to a validated attendance record, including corrections. Use the result to plan staffing and appointment slots. If a trial takes an average of several minutes per person, multiply the measured time by the expected intake and account for the number of trained stations; do not borrow a vendor's best-case demonstration time.

Check user and licence limits, simultaneous camera streams, available storage, retention settings and export capacity with the actual supplier. At a crowded entrance, test the intended movement pattern and normal lighting. More people in a view can change occlusion and processing requirements even when the camera itself is unchanged.

Follow the system-specific [face-enrolment checklist](/insights/how-many-photos-face-enrolment) where face attendance is proposed. Microsoft's [enrolment guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/face/enrollment-overview) emphasizes an understandable capture process and responsible handling of user information; it is not a prescribed PGAK photo count or throughput figure.

## Design a safe fallback before the first queue

Do not obstruct a busy entrance or delay necessary site induction just to complete a technology demonstration. Agree where registration can happen safely and how attendance will be recorded when a worker has not completed it. A controlled manual record can be a legitimate temporary fallback if it identifies the person, time, recorder and later reviewer.

Give each unresolved case a reason and next action. Distinguish an unregistered worker from a failed match, a missing event and a roster error. Otherwise every problem becomes the same vague instruction to enter attendance by hand.

Where protective equipment is required, keep it in place during the relevant trial. If the proposed workflow cannot operate acceptably under those conditions, agree a supported alternative instead of asking workers to compromise site safety.

## Close the season as carefully as it starts

Confirm the final attendance period, outstanding corrections and the owner of any later enquiry. Deactivate operational access according to the approved exit process without deleting records needed for an unresolved matter. HR and the responsible compliance team should determine retention and disposal requirements for the specific establishment and data involved.

Keep the dates and reasons for reactivation or deactivation. A former worker's profile should not remain available for routine attendance merely because nobody has received an exit list. Equally, a returning worker should not inherit an unrelated person's record to make setup faster.

## Common questions

### Is enrolment always the main seasonal attendance bottleneck?

No. Registration, identity mapping, camera coverage, processing capacity, licence limits and exception review can each constrain the rollout. Test the expected peak conditions before deciding what needs to change.

### Can a controlled manual record be used during setup?

Yes, as an agreed fallback with clear identity, date, recorder and review responsibility. Reconcile it promptly and preserve the correction history rather than presenting a later digital entry as an original automatic observation.
