## Someone needs the clip today

A customer says a parcel went missing. A manager wants to see a gate at 9:40 pm. A police station or an insurer asks for a copy. You have a recorder full of footage and a USB drive in your pocket. How do you get the right clip out without damaging anything?

This guide covers a general export for most DVRs and NVRs. Menus differ by brand, so keep your recorder's manual or the maker's support page open.

## Before you export: protect the original

The recorder overwrites its oldest footage when the disk is full. If the clip matters, do the first two things below before anything else.

1. Note the exact date, time and camera name. Write them down.
2. Export the clip at once. Do not wait for a convenient day, because the original may be overwritten.
3. Ask your installer whether the recorder can lock or protect a time range, if your model supports it.

[How many days of CCTV footage you can keep](/insights/cctv-footage-storage-how-many-days) explains why old footage disappears, and [the CCTV storage calculator](/calculators/cctv-storage) helps you estimate it.

## Step 1: Pick a suitable USB drive

Use a clean drive you control. Check its free space, because video files are large. If the recorder asks for a specific format, such as FAT32, follow the manual. Some recorders cannot read very large drives, so a smaller one often works better.

Do not use a drive that holds other people's files.

## Step 2: Find the export or backup menu

Log in to the recorder as a user allowed to export. Look for a menu named Backup, Export or Archive. Plug the drive into the recorder's USB port and wait for it to be detected.

If the recorder will not detect the drive, try another port or another drive before you assume the recorder is faulty.

## Step 3: Choose the camera and the exact time

Select only the cameras and the time range you need, plus a few minutes before and after the event. A narrow range exports faster and is easier to review. Exporting the whole archive takes a long time and fills the drive.

Check the file size estimate if the recorder shows one, and confirm it fits.

## Step 4: Check the file format

Some recorders save a common format such as MP4 or AVI. Others save their own format that needs a special player. If the export includes a player program, copy it onto the drive too. Without it, the person you hand the clip to may not be able to open it.

## Step 5: Play the clip on a computer before you hand it over

This step is easy to skip, and it causes most trouble. Plug the drive into a computer and play the file. Check that:

- the right camera and time are shown
- the clip starts a little before the event
- the on-screen time looks right for the event
- the picture is smooth and has the quality you expect

If the recorder clock was wrong, write that down. A note on how far off it was helps whoever reviews it. [Why timestamps matter for a dispute](/insights/cctv-timestamps-match-dispatch-records) explains what to check.

## Step 6: Keep a copy and a short record

Keep one copy of the exported file on a safe computer, and write a short note: who exported it, when, which camera and which time range. Hand over a copy, not your only one.

If the footage may be used in a legal matter, ask a lawyer how to handle it. [CCTV footage as evidence in India](/insights/cctv-footage-legal-evidence-india) gives general background, not legal advice.

## Who should see the footage?

Footage shows people. Share it only with those who have a real need, and note whom you gave it to. [Who can view CCTV footage at work](/insights/who-can-view-cctv-footage-at-work) covers sensible access rules.

## If export fails

If the export fails again, do not format the recorder's disk or reset it. Call your installer. A failed export can point to a faulty USB port, a full drive or a recorder fault. [DVR or NVR: which do you have](/insights/dvr-vs-nvr-which-do-you-have) helps you tell the installer what you own.

If you review a lot of footage by hand, the feasibility check can look at whether searching by event could suit your cameras. That depends on your setup, and it is a conversation, not a promise.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I copy CCTV footage from a DVR to a USB drive?

Plug a USB drive into the recorder, open its backup or export menu, choose the camera and the exact start and end time, then start the export. Menu names differ by brand, so check your recorder's manual for the model.

### Why will my CCTV footage not play on my computer?

Some recorders export in their own file format that needs the maker's player. Look for a player program on the USB drive, or choose a common format such as MP4 in the export menu if your recorder offers it. Test the file on a computer before you hand over the drive.

### Will exporting footage delete it from the recorder?

Normally no. An export makes a copy and leaves the recording where it is. The recorder still overwrites old footage when the disk fills, so export soon, and ask your installer if the original needs to be locked.
