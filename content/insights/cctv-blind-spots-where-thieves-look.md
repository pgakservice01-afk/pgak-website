A floor plan covered with camera symbols can still leave important questions unanswered. Which view shows the delivery handover? Can the recorded image reveal the detail needed at the gate? What happens when a truck or stack of material blocks that view?

A coverage review turns those questions into a test. Its purpose is to verify defined tasks in real operating conditions, not to claim that every part of the site must identify every person at all times.

## Define the job for each area

Start with the site's operating requirements. Mark entrances, handover points, restricted areas and the paths relevant to the project. Write what you need to observe at each point.

Keep the tasks separate. Seeing that a person is present, following a movement and identifying useful detail are different requirements. A wide overview may be appropriate for one and inadequate for another.

For analytics, specify the event and the area in which it must be evaluated. The camera view should be assessed against the application's requirements. Manufacturer documentation such as [Axis Object Analytics guidance](https://help.axis.com/en-us/axis-object-analytics) describes installation and scene considerations for that product; apply the requirements of the actual proposed system.

## Mark the view, not just the camera

On the plan, connect each task to a camera identifier and approximate field of view. Mark permanent obstructions such as walls, pillars and equipment. Then add movable obstructions that appear during normal work.

Ask the people who use the area where pallets, vehicles and temporary displays are normally placed. A drawing based on an empty building can miss the condition that exists for most of the working day.

Keep a short issue register:

| Area | Required task | Observed limitation | Next test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery position | Review a handover | Vehicle blocks the view | Test an alternative position |
| Entrance | Review movement through the door | Bright background hides detail | Compare relevant lighting conditions |
| Restricted zone | Evaluate an entry rule | Stored material obscures the boundary | Recheck the agreed storage layout |

These are example planning entries, not findings about your premises.

## Verify against recordings

Arrange an authorised walk-through with a person watching the live view and another recording the test notes. Use normal operating routes and safe procedures. Note the time and camera identifier for each observation.

Then retrieve the recording. Confirm that the detail needed for the task remains available in the saved footage, at the configured settings. A good live display does not establish the quality or availability of the retained recording.

Repeat relevant checks under different lighting or operating conditions. If a condition cannot be tested yet, label it untested and schedule it rather than marking the area approved by assumption.

## Fix the cause before increasing the count

Potential responses include adjusting the view, changing lighting, controlling an obstruction or adding an appropriately specified camera. Each change should address a recorded gap in a defined task.

Moving a camera can improve one view while weakening another. Recheck the original requirement as well as the new one after an adjustment. If another camera is proposed, ask why the existing views cannot meet the task and what the additional view will demonstrate.

The [resolution and distance guide](/insights/camera-resolution-vs-distance) explains why the camera's headline resolution alone does not settle the question.

## Keep the plan current

Review the coverage record when the layout, racking, vehicle routes or operating process changes. Assign someone to record material changes and request a recheck. Keep sensitive floor plans and access details within the authorised project team.

## Common questions

### Does a camera pointing at an area mean it covers the task?

No. A view may show that someone is present without showing the detail needed for identification or a particular analytics event. Test the required task in the recorded image.

### Should every blind spot be fixed by adding another camera?

No. First assess the cause and requirement. A change to the view, lighting or obstruction may help; another camera may be appropriate when the existing view cannot meet the defined task.
